TransportPipes · v1.3 plan · after your first look at the test server

Your Own Place

You start with nothing but your land. You choose where you live by clicking a spot on the map. You can trade with the market alone, or with friends at prices the seller sets. No computer-run companies share the map with you, and the land and the Yard get much bigger.

Start from scratchAn HQ, a trading post and 2,000 gold. You build everything else yourself.
Pick your spotThe start screen is the map. Click a free plot, next to a friend or anywhere you like.
Crews that growFound a crew whenever you want, and anyone can join it later. There are no held seats and no codes.
Room to buildPlots of 48×48 tiles (2.25 times today's land) and a Yard of 30×20 (six times today's).
Only peopleHouses and stand-ins are gone. A plot nobody lives on is simply free land.
Your own pricesThe market always buys and sells. Friends beat it, at prices the seller sets, with special prices for whoever they like.

2 · Start from scratch

A new company gets its HQ and its trading post (what you pipe to the post is sold to the market), plus 2,000 gold. It also gets a plot whose land it saw before choosing it. Nothing else is built.

The first minutes don't get slower. A Wind Moily costs 200 gold and makes 30 energy a minute, worth 30 gold at the market. 2,000 gold buys several, and four of them with their pipes already make the kit's 100 gold a minute within the first five minutes, except that you built them, where you wanted them.

  • Loady's first chapter already starts from nothing: a panel, the first pipe and sale, an upgrade, a moily, the Yard, a mine. The rule that its first two steps ignore the kit goes, together with the kit.
  • The bots (a test tool, never in the game) measure the first hour from nothing: when the first sale comes and when 100 gold a minute comes. If the start drags, the starting gold changes, not the kit.

3 · Pick your spot

1Open the gameOr a friend's link: it opens straight at their place.
2The mapThe real world map, zoomed out. Free plots glow. Each taken one shows its company, crew banner and level.
3A plot's cardIts land (wind, sun, deposits and its one strength), the neighbours (level, crew, last played), and the way to the hub.
4Name and colourYour company, as today.
5PlayAs a guest if you like. You can keep the estate with a name and password later.
  • Find a player: a name search on the map.
  • Pick for me: a free plot in a Yard where people played in the last three days (today's "Find me neighbours").
  • Your link: every player has one, "Play next to me". It opens the map at your plot, with the free plots of your Yard and the Yards around it marked. Nothing is reserved: the first to come gets the plot. When your Yard is full, the nearest free plots are marked instead.
  • A crew's Yard: when one crew lives on three of a Yard's four plots, the fourth is for its members unless the crew opens it. This is today's rule, kept, so a friend who comes later still finds room next to you.
  • Moving later works on the same map: pick a free plot. Moving stays free, with a few minutes between moves.
  • Guests stay as they are. No account is needed. A chat app's browser asks for one at once, because it forgets guests.

Goes: the start screen's three ways, seat codes and held seats, the invite screen, "Settle and invite my friends", the 28-day holds, the keep cards and the "Move to a fresh station together?" card.

4 · Crews that grow

  • You found a crew in the game, at any time from Level 1: a name and a banner. The start screen never asks for one.
  • Joining: any member invites a player (anyone, anywhere), or a player asks and any member lets them in. When you settle next to a friend who has a crew, the game asks once: "Join Ana's crew?" Leaving takes one tap.
  • No limit on members. A Yard still holds four plots. A crew bigger than that lives in Yards next to each other and trades over links.
  • A crew gives you: its chat, its banner on the members' plots, the crew price (§6), the crew's news, crew trunks in Era 2, and "where we live": the members' plots, each with Go there.

5 · Room to build

  • Plots of 48×48: still 16 parcels, now 12×12 tiles each, with the same unlock order, levels and prices. You get more land for the same gold. The start area of four parcels is 24×24.
  • A Yard of 30×20 at every crossing of four plots: three times as wide and twice as high as today, so wider than high. The two-plot Yards on the hub's arms become 30×20 as well. A district grows from 172 to 252 tiles across, and the hub city's block from 32×32 to 48×48.
  • Land for everyone: every start area has small deposits of the Era 1 basics (iron, copper ore, coal and stone), water, a little forest and a little beach, so you can play Era 1 alone. Each plot also has one strength: a rich deposit, strong wind, strong sun, a big beach (sand) or old forest (wood). The plot's card shows it before you choose. Dealing out the land per station, where no plot could run a chain alone, goes.
  • Speed: a full 48×48 plot holds about 2.25 times as many buildings (around 500). Today, a build in a grown plot (87 buildings) reaches the map in 0.06–0.16 seconds. At 216 buildings, solving the network alone takes 85–157 ms. Step 2 measures a full plot with the big-estate script against 1.5 seconds on a laptop running 4× slower. If it's over, the solve gets split up.

6 · Only people, and your own prices

No computer-run companies

  • A free plot is free land, nothing more. Your Yard trades between the people who live in it. What nobody takes goes to the market through your trading post, and what nobody sells you, the market sells.
  • Players who stop: nothing changes except the stand-in. On day 23 their partners are told. From day 30 the estate rests where it is and trades nothing. On day 90 it travels to the Quiet Isles and the plot is free again. Back again, they pick a new spot.
  • Quiet servers keep their bigger Hub Orders: that is the market being generous, not a player.

The market, and friends who beat it

The market pays you the base price (less once you sell beyond your Hub Order) and charges 2.5× the base. Any trade between those two prices is better for both sides than the market.

  • Your prices: for every good you sell in the Yard, you set a price between what the market pays and what it charges (1× to 2.5× the base). It starts at today's 1.3×, so nothing changes until you change it.
  • Special prices for your crew and for named players: "Ben: 1.0×".
  • Buying: a building that lacks a good takes it from the cheapest offer it can reach (your Yard, then your links), and from the market last. When there isn't enough for everyone, a seller can put a player first, as Send ▸ does today.
  • Price rises wait an hour, with a note to the buyers. Cuts count at once.
  • Links, contracts and the Exchange use the same prices.

Each price sits between the market's two prices, so a trade with a friend is never worse than the market for either side. The guards against one person running two accounts stay.

7 · A Yard worth the room

  • The project in the middle: the Foundation, the Water Tower and the Landmark's storeys get room to stand and grow. Today they sit in a corner of the Yard.
  • A stall at every gate, on the Yard's edge, with a price sign. The goods the Yard trades are drawn moving across the paving from the seller's stall to the buyer's.
  • Decorate together: neighbours place today's decorations (trees, flower beds, lamps, fountains, statues) in the Yard, where everyone in the station sees them. This also gives gold a place to go.

9 · What I expect to go wrong

Friends may each build everything and never tradeThat was the first playtest's failure, and dealing out the land was the answer to it. Now the answer is the gap between the market's two prices: a friend's 1.3× beats the market's 2.5× for the buyer and its 1× for the seller. The bots check this in step 8. If crews don't come out ahead, the plots' strengths get stronger.
  • An empty Yard on a quiet server feels quiet. That's the choice. Pick for me puts a newcomer next to people who play.
  • Your own prices bring a little haggling, and now and then a greedy neighbour. The market's own price limits every price, and you can always buy from the market instead.
  • Bigger plots mean bigger networks. This is measured in step 2, before anything else is built on it.
  • A lot of code and tests go (Houses, stand-ins, seats, dealt land, kits). Several e2e scripts retire: houses, evening, crew, seat-race, friend-late, land.
  • The project's keystones ("the good nobody here makes") came with dealt land. They still work without it, but their baskets need tuning again.
