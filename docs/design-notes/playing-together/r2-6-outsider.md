# Round 2 · The outsider: Four Seats, a Yard and Honest Pipes

I read the five other files and the brief. This round has five parts: the assumptions the insiders share, the five
weakest ideas (two of them mine), the five strongest, one revised design walked through six scenarios, and a score
against the brief's 14 points.

Names: Ana, Ben, Cleo and Dev are the friends (as in 2-friends). Lina plays alone, Ravi starts late and Gary is the
griefer. A season is taken as 4 weeks (28 days) in a persistent world: the world carries on between seasons, and each
season brings a new era chapter and a new Wonder.

---

## 1. Assumptions the insiders share that might be wrong

### A1. "Playing together means trading goods for gold"

Every file builds a price ladder: a crew price of 1.75×, positions in a price band, a station market at 1.4/2.0,
trust levels, tolls, presets. The insiders treat cooperation as accounting.

**If it's wrong:** three friends don't remember that Ben sold Ana coal at 1.75×. They remember building the Water
Tower together. A fixed crew price then becomes an invisible income buff. Players stop noticing whom they trade with,
which is the same anonymity that killed the district network. The social layer has numbers but no moments.

**What follows:** the centre should be a shared *object* everyone pipes into: the Yard, its works, a trunk line with
names on it. Trade between friends gets one fixed, automatic price that nobody ever has to think about. Sliders and a
second market tier go.

### A2. "There will be enough players to fill the structures"

The proposals assume a crowd: Quads and Duos and Streets, leagues of 25, 6–12 Houses per district, Hosts, Frontier
districts, rental markets for trunk slots, a Great Migration matched by level band and hours, districts grouped by
playing hours. Testers are playing now, which probably means tens of players, not thousands.

**If it's wrong:** most structures stand empty and get filled with NPCs, so the world becomes mostly bots with a few
humans scattered among them. Matching by level and hours has nobody to match. Leagues of 25 have 4 members.

**What follows:** put players close together before adding anything decorative. The world opens only as many stations
as it needs, and placement puts real players next to each other. Every feature must work with 5 players before it may
exist, and NPCs are the exception rather than the background.

### A3. "NPCs with personality can stand in for people"

Lodgers with cakes, Houses with letters, moods and a Rival, successor Houses, a Guild of Independents led by the Mayor.

**If it's wrong:** scripted warmth wears off in a week. A 30-line text table is fully read by day 6. Letters turn into
inbox noise that pushes out the few real messages, and a rival that always stays 5–10% ahead is plainly rubber-banded.
The lonely player feels managed instead of accompanied.

**What follows:** NPCs are honest tools: buyers, seat-warmers and stand-ins with a name and a little charm. They are
never the social layer. The effort goes into showing the *traces of real players* (the Death Stranding template in
5-lessons): their pipes, trunk lines and favours.

### A4. "A seat is a role"

2-friends has complementary seats where "Ben takes coal" at minute 4, 1-wild has professions, 4-economy has home goods.
The insiders assume specialisation is fun from the first session.

**If it's wrong:** the role gets chosen before anyone understands the game. The coal player has the dullest game
(dozens of identical mines). And a role is a dependency: when the coal player leaves, the crew's main input is a
Lodger at 30%.

**What follows:** seats differ in *land* (a mild ×1.5–2 on two goods) and are named after their land, not a job.
Every seat can build every chain. Specialisation should appear in week 2, when tier-3 goods become scarce at the hub
(4-economy M8), not at registration.

### A5. "Players look at the map"

Banners, sleeping cats, sparkles, the Crew Lens, Wish balloons: nearly all the social signals live on the district
map. A 3-minute phone session goes: open, read the report, collect, build one thing, close.

**If it's wrong:** most of those signals are never seen.

**What follows:** social state goes where the player already looks. First the opening report, then the edge of their
own estate. That is the best argument for putting the Yard at every member's inner corner: it's on screen whenever
you look at your own land.

(A sixth one runs through all five files: *more layers mean more depth.* Crew + Quad + Duo + Street + corporation +
station market + links + contracts + trunk slots + trust + mastery + leagues + chronicle + Houses is a lot of nouns
for a game played a few minutes a day. My rule below allows four.)

## 2. The five weakest ideas, and how each one fails

### W1. The station market at the junction (4-economy M4, Model C)

Each estate pipes a market line to the junction. The junction pairs every seller with every buyer, pro rata, at
1.4/2.0.

**Failure:** in Ashford NE the station market sells Ana's coal automatically at 1.4. A direct link pays only 25% more
and needs a decision, so nobody links. After a week Ana can't say who buys her coal. This is the district
mega-network the owner wanted gone, rebuilt at station scale with an extra price tier. It also brings a six-step
flow order ("why did my coal sell at 1.4 and not 1.75?") that no player can answer from the screen. In a quiet
station the "market" is mostly tenants, which is the hub with a different number.

### W2. Seats as fixed roles at minute four (2-friends #2; also 1-wild #39 professions)

**Failure:** Ben "obviously" takes Coal Valley. By day 5 he has 30 coal mines and wants to build steel. If he burns
his own coal, Cleo's Glassworks and Ana's Smelter run short, and the crew ticker helpfully says "Cleo's Smelter has
been out of coal since 3:10. Ben has 2,000 coal in storage." The game has turned his own choice into guilt. And if
Ben quits on day 3, the crew's core input drops to a Lodger at 30%.

### W3. Houses as the world's population (3-lonely 1.1 and 7.1)

Up to 10–12 Houses per district, archetypes, daily letters, a Rival.

**Failure:** on a 5-player server, Lina's district is 5 humans and 12 Houses. Her inbox gets four letters a day from
Brassworth, Madame Ferro and the Curio Guild, and by day 6 she has seen every line. Kestrel Industrial is "always
5–10% ahead", which is obviously faked, so she either stops caring or feels cheated. The cost is growth scripts per
archetype, estate templates, art and text: a week of work, not 2–3 days. The district looks busy and still feels
empty.

### W4. Headcount rewards

This covers my own **Quartet Recipes** (four human inputs), 2-friends' **diversity bonus** (×1.0/1.2/1.35/1.5 by the
number of contributors) and 1-wild's professions that only pay when trading with a different profession.

**Failure:** Ben quits on day 3. From then on the crew can never make a Quartet item, and loses 15% of every weekly
reward because only Ana and Cleo deliver. Every week the contribution ring shows Ben's colour missing. Ana's natural
next thought is "should we replace Ben?": kick drama, the Egg Inc. and Hay Day failure. And Gary, with three accounts
on three devices, gets the ×1.5. This is FarmVille's "you need more friends" wall in nicer words. I drop the Quartet
Recipes.

### W5. My own Great Pipe as one world spine (also 1-wild #24)

**Failure at both scales:** with 5 players, it's a progress bar that one crew fills in two days. With 2,000 players,
Lina's share is 0.003%, and big crews finish each segment before she logs in. And the brief shows the game already
has a Wonder every season, so it would be a second Wonder with a pipe painted on it. I replace it with local trunk
lines from each station *to* the existing Wonder site (the Wonder relay, below).

**Also weak, briefly:**
- The accordion map (1-wild #7) re-packs the world at night and breaks layouts and spatial memory. Relationships need
  stable neighbours, as 5-lessons notes about Forge of Empires.
- The trunk-slot rental market (4-economy M9) is a rental economy for a population that doesn't exist yet.
- Live cursors (2-friends #4) are a feature for two people being online at once, which almost never happens.
- My own Echoes are an opaque fake partner.
- My own Shift Log coverage bonus is a streak in disguise.

## 3. The five strongest ideas, and what makes them strong

### S1. Honest link states, and a successor that inherits the link (3-lonely 3.1–3.2)

"Every zero has a sentence." Each link shows one of five states (live, resting, caretaker, handed over, capped), and
a successor keeps about 90% of the old trade going. It turns churn into onboarding: the leaver's partner keeps the
trade, and the newcomer who takes the seat finds a proven, active human partner in minute one. It removes the worst
feeling in the game, the silent dead pipe, with almost no new system: a status per link plus a tenant that copies
volumes.

### S2. Pack, don't wreck: the deed and the moving van (1-wild 6+29, 5-lessons 6–7, 3-lonely 4.1)

The whole estate is stored as a blueprint plus its storage, then unpacked in another seat. That one mechanism handles
the friend who joins late, the wrong neighbours, the griefer ("you can always leave"), the leaver who blocks land, the
return after a month and the migration of the testers' world. It reuses the blueprint feature that already exists. No
other idea covers as many cases per day of work.

### S3. An invite link that holds a seat, warmed by a Lodger that hands over its works (2-friends #1 and #10, 5-lessons #2)

It makes "three friends tonight, a fourth next week" a planned path. The late friend arrives in a furnished estate as
the missing piece, with copper the crew has been buying from the hub. It turns the hardest moment, a friend arriving
far behind the others, into a celebration.

### S4. Lines, not webs, with tanks and a hub fallback (1-wild 21+14, 4-economy M5 and M11)

Every pipe between two companies has two named ends and a manifest. A tank at the buyer's gate buffers it, and the hub
steps in when there is a shortfall. This fixes the root cause of the mega-network while keeping pipes as the way
partners express a partnership. "An idle partner must not stall you" stops being a promise and becomes something the
system can't break.

### S5. Banked one-tap favours, tending and Wishes (5-lessons #5, 3-lonely 2.3, 2-friends #8)

They're cheap, asynchronous and can only help. "Ana tended your estate 17 times while you were away" is the strongest
win-back line in any of the files. Favours give a 3-minute session a social action, and they give absence a warm
meaning instead of a guilty one. Banking them for 3 days means a missed day costs nothing.

**Honourable mentions:**
- The personal hub allowance (4-economy M7): nobody can move anybody else's price.
- Credit measured as a share of your *own* output (3-lonely 5.4), plus per-player caps (5-lessons).
- The Yard carved at every member's inner corner (2-friends).
- The Death Stranding rule for lonely players (5-lessons).

## 4. The revised design: Four Seats, a Yard and Honest Pipes

### 4.1 Six rules (the outsider's guard rails)

1. **Four nouns:** Station, Link, Caretaker, Favour. (The people of a station are its *crew*. Corporations stay as the
   existing layer for big groups.)
2. **It works with 5 players first.** No feature that needs a crowd to make sense.
3. **Nothing scales with the number of humans.** Goals scale to *active* members, rewards to your *own* effort. A
   missing friend is never a penalty.
4. **Goods, not gold, between friends.** One fixed price, no sliders, no gifts of gold.
5. **Real traces over fake people.** NPCs are labelled tools with a little charm.
6. **Social state lives where you already look:** the opening report, then the Yard at the corner of your own
   estate.

### 4.2 The station: the place you choose

- **Geometry (taken from 2-friends):** the four corner 2×2 groups of a district become stations of 4 seats (16
  seats). The 8 estates on the arms become Duos (2 seats with a small Yard) for couples, or for a fifth friend next to
  a full station. The Yard is the 18×18 plaza made of the four inner-corner parcels (the parcel bought last, so almost
  nobody has built there) plus the corridor crossing. Each estate gets a Yard gate at its inner corner.
- **Seats differ in land, not in job.** Each seat has two home goods (★5 ×2.0 and ★4 ×1.5) and small ★2 deposits of
  every other basic (4-economy M1). A seat is called "Coal Valley", never "the coal seat". Every chain is possible
  everywhere.
- **Three ways in** on the start screen:

  | Button | What happens |
  | --- | --- |
  | **Start with friends** | Pick a crew name, emblem and colours, and get an invite link. The seat map shows fresh stations. Each friend drags their banner onto a seat. Unclaimed seats are *reserved* for 7 days (renewable once) and kept warm by a Lodger. |
  | **Find me neighbours** (the default) | Puts you in a station with active humans whose usual hours match yours. Only when no such seat exists does it open a fresh station. |
  | **A station of my own** | Three Lodgers fill the other seats. You can open them to players later. |

- **The seat map card** shows: the residents (name, level, *lit windows* = played today or this week, usual hours,
  in 4 buckets), the land of each free seat, what the station imports from the hub ("buys 1,100 copper a day"), and
  whether the station is open, closed, or has reserved seats.
- **Density first (rule 2):** a new district opens only when 75% of the seats in existing districts are taken by
  active players. A deed frees its seat, so dormant estates don't count. With 5 players there is 1 district with 2
  stations in use. With 2,000 there are about 100 districts.
- **Moving is cheap:** a free move as a deed in your first 3 days, then one free move a week, at any time. A choice you
  can undo is a choice you dare to make.
- **Tailwind (one catch-up rule for everyone):** XP ×2 (×2.5 from week 3 of a season) while you are more than 3 levels
  below the active median of your station (of the server, for a station of your own). It ends by itself, without a
  timer. Late friends, late newcomers and returners all get the same rule.

### 4.3 The Yard: the shared object

- **The Crew Depot** stands in the Yard from the start. Loady's fifth tutorial step, around minute 12: "lay a pipe
  from your Yard gate to the Depot (6 tiles)". That pipe is your **Crew Line**, and each member has exactly one. When
  the second line arrives, the **Handshake** plays: both pipes flash in their owners' colours and both players get a
  small gift. It works asynchronously: if the other line was laid yesterday, the handshake plays when yours connects.
- **The crew network** is the only network that spans several estates, and it is capped at the 4 seats (2 in a Duo).
  Goods move between members at the **crew price, the middle of the band (1.75× hub base), fixed and automatic.**
- **Flow order for a producer:** own consumers → links and contracts (the things you chose) → crew → storage → hub.
  A per-resource switch "keep for myself" exists, off by default.
- **Nobody builds in the Yard.** Its buildings belong to the station and grow from Station Orders. Members own only
  their Crew Line. So a stranger can't rearrange or wreck it.
- **Readable from your own estate:** the Crew Line has a manifest ("this week: Ben → Ana 12,400 coal · Ana → Cleo
  31,000 ⚡"), and goods roll through the Yard in their senders' colours. The report opens with the crew lines
  (rule 6).

### 4.4 Station Orders and the Works: building something together

- **Orders:** a new Station Order arrives every 8 hours, and up to 3 wait. **They never expire.** Each one is at most
  ~45 minutes of one member's output, so one person can always finish one.
- **Goal size scales to active members.** Caretakers and Lodgers count as 0.5 and deliver slowly by themselves, so even
  a station with one human finishes its goals.
- **Credit and rewards:**
  - Personal credit is the share of *your own* output you delivered, capped per day.
  - The full reward needs a small floor: 3% of the goal or 1 hour of your own output, whichever is smaller. Below it
    you get a 25% "thanks" share.
  - The station perk (e.g. +5% production) goes to everyone and costs nobody anything.
  - The ring around the project shows colours, never a "who did least" list.
- **The Yard grows visibly:** Water Tower → Yard Storage → Workshop (+5%) → shared outpost licence → **Landmark**, which
  can be seen from the district map.
- **The Works' last stage** is a sustained flow (4-economy M12): e.g. "800 steel/min for 6 hours", which one estate
  can't manage and a few can. It stays asynchronous because tanks and storages buffer it.

### 4.5 Links between stations: honest pipes

- **Lines, not webs.** A link joins two gates of two named companies and is never transitive. It is auto-routed
  through corridors, and many links can share a corridor (drawn as a bundle), so nobody can own or block a corridor
  tile.
- Each link has a **manifest**, a **tank** at the buyer's gate (2 hours of flow), a **1-hour notice** before a pause
  or a price change, and a **hub fallback** when it runs short.
- **The price is one of three presets inside the band:** Friend (25%), Fair (50%, the default) or Premium (75%). There
  is no free slider.
- **Every link always shows one honest status line** (S1): live, resting, caretaker, handed over, or capped ("No one
  at this gate yet. Your coal goes to the hub meanwhile").
- **Two mechanics push players outward when they're ready:**
  - A **personal hub allowance** (M7): above it, the hub pays you less for your main product.
  - **Tier-3+ goods are scarce at the hub** (M8).

  So in week 2 links between stations become worth it. Nobody can move another player's price.
- **Verdict on "direct trade only":** keep it, with two exceptions. The bounded crew network inside a station is one;
  trunk lines that *carry* a link without merging networks are the other. Goods never pass through a third
  company's estate.

### 4.6 Trunk lines to the Wonder: the late game, built with pipes

- From Crew Level 6, a station can build a **trunk line** from its Yard to the season's Wonder site, or to a shared
  wilderness outpost.
- It is built in 10-tile segments, and **each segment is striped in the colour of the member who paid for it.**
- A **pump house** every 25 tiles can be *adopted* by a member, who upgrades it and puts their banner on it. An
  unadopted pump still works at base level, so no absence breaks the line.
- **The Wonder relay:** the Wonder's top-tier parts must arrive by pipe. Over a season the map shows the stations'
  trunk lines converging on the Wonder: a picture of the whole world cooperating.
- **Public once built:** any station within 10 tiles can connect its Yard to a finished trunk for free. Its deliveries
  are its own, and the builders get a thank-you and a "carried 41,000 steel for 6 stations" plate. Strangers' work
  helps you, and all you can do is thank them (the Death Stranding template).
- No tolls and no slot rental in the first version. Add them only if the population ever makes them meaningful.

### 4.7 Caretakers and deeds: absence without harm

| Away | What others see | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–3 days | Nothing | Normal |
| 3–14 days | A sleeping cat on the HQ steps (cute, vague, never "last seen 9 d") | The estate runs as built, and its links and crew flows continue. When storages are full, the overflow sells at 70% of hub price into a **savings jar** (capped at 7 days of income). Station goals resize. |
| 14–30 days | A caretaker hat on the gate | The caretaker honours links and contracts at their last terms and renews good contracts. It never builds or demolishes. |
| 30+ days | The banner moves to the Yard's crew wall | The estate is **packed into a deed** (blueprint + storage + levels). A **successor Lodger** takes the seat and copies 90% of the last 7 days of trade, so no friend's Smelter goes cold. By default the seat is held for the owner for 60 days, and the crew can open it earlier. |

- **The return** is three screens in under a minute:
  1. *While you were away*, at most 5 items.
  2. *Your estate is safe.*
  3. *Where to land*: your held seat, next to a friend, a lively station, or the newest district.

  Landing is free, links are offered back, and each missed era comes with a jump kit (a blueprint of the standard
  chain plus a research discount) **that never expires**.
- **At most three win-back messages per absence**, all concrete and positive, never a countdown.

### 4.8 Favours: the 3-minute social action

- **3 favours a day, which bank up to 9:**
  - **Tune-up:** +10% on one building for 8 hours.
  - **Tend** a resting estate: a cosmetic, +2% for a day, and a counter the owner sees on return.
  - **Fill a Wish:** one tap, "my spare coal goes to Cleo first for 24 h".
  - **Leave a crate:** at most 1 hour of your own production of one good.
- Favours work inside your station and along your links, so a solo player gives and receives them too. The receiver
  sees a thank-you button, and the giver earns a "Good Neighbour" count.
- **A Wish** is a request pinned to your Yard gate. It shows in your crew's report and to the stations you have links
  with. Unfilled Wishes simply fade after 3 days, and nobody is told who didn't help.

### 4.9 Recognition and friendly competition

- **The report first:** the crew lines, favours received, Wishes, then one line about the world ("Fox & Sons connected
  their trunk to the Observatory").
- **A Station Story** each week: a postcard of the Yard with the banners, the Landmark's progress and three highlights.
  It can be shared outside the game, with an invite link attached.
- **Names on things:** trunk segments, pump houses, the Works plaque, the Landmark.
- **The Station Cup** ranks stations by deliveries per active member, counting each member's best 3 days. It runs among
  stations at a similar stage, or among all stations when there are few. Below a dozen stations it falls back to
  "your best week".
- **Mastery titles** come from the season's total per good ("Master Glassmaker of Ashford").
- **No global ranking** is ever shown to a player in their first two weeks.

### 4.10 Honest NPCs

- A **Lodger** is a labelled NPC company (a cog badge) with a name, a portrait and 4–5 lines of personality. It runs
  modest works for its land, trades in the Yard at the crew price, posts the occasional Wish and sends one thank-you a
  week at most.
- It is capped at about 30% of an active player's trade by week 2. When a human takes the seat, it hands its works over
  intact and leaves.
- There are no Rival Houses, no daily letters and no NPC-led guild. The tenants that exist today keep buying.

### 4.11 What a 3-minute session looks like

1. **Report (30 s):** "Ben's coal kept your Smelter running all night. Cleo wishes for glass. Dev tuned up your
   Foundry."
2. **One tap:** fill Cleo's Wish. One tap: tend Ben's estate (resting).
3. **One minute of building** on your own land. The Yard is at your corner, so you see the colours flowing.
4. **Leave.** Nothing waits for you. Nobody is worse off if you don't come back tomorrow.

### (b) A solo player on a server of 5 players

The server has Kettle Works (Ana, Cleo and Dev active, Ben resting) in Ashford NE, Mo alone in Ashford NW, and now
Lina, who installs the game at 03:00 her time. There is one district and two stations in use (density first).

1. **Start:** Lina taps the default, **Find me neighbours**. Kettle Works is full (Ben's seat is held). The seat map
   recommends Ashford NW: "Mo · Lv 9 · lit today · usually mornings · Riverside and Old Forest free · imports glass".
   She takes Riverside. The other two NW seats hold Lodgers.
2. **Minute 12:** Lina's Crew Line reaches the Depot. Mo's line was laid days ago, so the Handshake plays at once:
   "Mo's Smelter bought your first 40 ⚡". Mo is asleep. The first social moment of the game needed nobody to be
   online.
3. **Her first night:** Station Orders are sized to 1 active human (Mo hasn't played for 18 hours, so he counts as
   active) plus 2 Lodgers at 0.5, so the first order takes her 20 minutes. Bertha's twin, a Lodger called Old Pell,
   buys her sand.
4. **Morning:** her report says "Mo tuned up your Glassworks (+10%) and thanked you for the energy." There is one line
   about the world: "Kettle Works started a trunk line to the Observatory." The world is small, but every name in it
   is a real person who did something.
5. **Week 2:** Kettle Works' trunk passes within 10 tiles of the NW Yard. Lina and Mo connect their Yard to it for
   free and send glass to the Wonder, and their deliveries count as theirs. A plate on the trunk says "carried glass for
   Ashford NW". Ana sees "Ashford NW joined your trunk" and taps thanks.
6. **Competition at 5 players:** the Station Cup has 2 stations, counted per active member over each member's best 3
   days, so Lina and Mo win a week against a full crew. Otherwise it shows "your best week".
7. **If Mo stops on day 12:** after 7 days, Lina gets one card, once: "Your neighbour is resting. **Stay** · **Move
   next to Kettle Works** (the Ashford N Duo, a 6-tile link to their gate) · **Open your station's seats** to new
   players." She stays. Mo's estate keeps supplying her through the Yard, and later his successor Lodger does. Nothing
   she built stalls.

**Honest limit:** at 03:00 on a 5-player server, Lina's game is a full solo game. The other people show up only as
traces (favours, trunk lines, one line in the report). That is right for an idle game, but it is not a party.

### (c) A newcomer in week 3, among veterans

Ravi starts on day 17. Most players are Level 22–30, and the Wonder is 60% built.

1. **Placement:** **Find me neighbours** scores the free seats. The best is an *open* seat in Fox & Sons (Ashford SE):
   "3 active, plays evenings CET, looking for sand · a successor Lodger holds the seat with a sand link to Mira's
   Glassworks". The second-best option shown is a fresh station in the newest district where four other week-3
   players started this week.
2. **Settle:** Fox & Sons opened the seat, so Ravi moves in directly. The successor Lodger hands over its works
   (Level 5 sand pits, a wind generator) and its trade: *"Mira's Glassworks already buys sand through your Yard: 900/h
   at the crew price."* His first gold comes from a real, active human in minute 8.
3. **Protection:** prices stay within the band, the crew price is fixed, nobody can offer him a contract with a
   shortfall clause below Level 6, and the hub allowance is personal, so no veteran's dumping touches his price.
4. **Catching up:**
   - Tailwind is XP ×2.5 from week 3 while he's below the median minus 3.
   - Research for techs most players already have is discounted (tech diffusion, which exists).
   - Each era comes with a jump kit (a blueprint of the standard chain).

   By day 24 he is Level 18.
5. **Mattering:** his Wonder and Station Order credit is the share of *his own* output delivered. At 20% he gets the
   same personal tier as a Level 30 player at 20%. The trunk line to the Wonder already exists, so on day 1 he sends
   sand toward a world project and sees his colour on the station's ring.
6. **Competition:** for his first two weeks he sees only the "Rookies" board and his station's Cup, never the global
   board.

### (d) A griefer tries to block or exploit others

Gary wants trouble. Here is every route he might try and where it stops.

| Gary tries | What stops him |
| --- | --- |
| Moving into the Kettle Works station to troll | Reserved seats are locked to invite links, and an open seat needs the crew's acceptance (or "move in directly", which a crew can switch off). |
| Wrecking the Yard after landing in a "Find me neighbours" station of strangers | He owns only his Crew Line. Yard buildings belong to the station and grow from Station Orders, so he can't build, move or demolish anything there. |
| Draining the crew's goods | Every draw is a purchase at the fixed crew price. That's a fair sale, not a leak. |
| Leeching Station Order rewards | Below the floor (3% of the goal or 1 h of his own output) he gets 25%. The station perk is a public good that costs nobody anything. |
| Walling off a corridor with pipes | Corridor tiles can't be owned. Links are auto-routed and share corridors as bundles. |
| Dumping goods to crash a rival's price | The hub allowance is personal. Dumping only lowers *his own* marginal price. |
| Funnelling gold with three alt accounts in one station | Value moves only as goods at fixed or band prices, and only for goods actually produced. An alt is worth exactly one real player's output and needs one real player's attention. Crates are capped at 1 hour of the giver's own production. Seats taken from one device don't count as "active members" for goal sizing. There is no headcount bonus to farm. |
| Cutting off a link at 3 a.m. to starve a partner | 1 h notice, a 2-hour tank and the hub fallback. On a contract he pays the difference between hub and contract price. |
| Spamming link requests | 5 per hour, a block list, and gates set to Closed. |
| An offensive name or banner | Emblems come from a preset set. Names are filtered and can be reported. Free-text chat exists only inside a crew, with mute. |
| Being toxic in the station chat | Everyone else can mute him. **Nobody can kick** (that would bring kick drama), but anyone can leave with a free move, and a crew can move together. Gary ends up alone with Lodgers. Three reports from different stations go to review. |

He has nothing to steal or break, and nobody is forced to stay near him. The worst he can do is be annoying in a chat
you can mute.

**Migration of the testers:**
- Each corner group of four estates becomes a station, and its residents become a crew they can name or leave with a
  free move.
- Buildings on the inner-corner parcels stay their owner's until moved. Anyone who built there gets a free deed move

### Main risks of the revised design

1. **The crew network turns friends' trade invisible.** It's automatic, so it can feel like infrastructure again. The
   manifest, the colours and the report lines are not decoration: if they're weak, the design fails at point 9 and at
   its own rule 4.
2. **Strangers placed together may clash.** That's the price of putting players next to each other. Nothing in the
   Yard can be edited, nobody can be kicked, and a free move is always one tap away, but a bad neighbour can still
   sour a station.
3. **The fixed station geometry.** If carving the Yard breaks too many testers' layouts or art, the fallback is
   2-friends' cleaner district v2: a 6×6 grid around a 2×2 hub, giving 8 stations, used for new districts only.
4. **A thin week 4 for optimisers.** Watch the testers. Add outposts and trunk tolls if they run out of goals, but not
   more NPCs.
