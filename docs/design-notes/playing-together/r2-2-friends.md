# Round 2 · Friends first, revised: stations, crews and links

Role: the friends-first designer. I read all six round-1 files and the brief. My position in five lines:

1. The small station around a shared plaza is the right *home*. But round 1 (mine included) overloaded it: it was
   where you live, who your friends are, who you trade with and what you build together, all at once. That is
   why it breaks for couples, groups of six, friends who fall out and strangers.
2. So the revised design splits it into three layers: the **station** (a place: 2 or 4 seats around a plaza), the
   **crew** (people: 1–8 friends, from Level 1, can span neighbouring stations) and **links** (trade: one pipe,
   two named companies, chosen, never transitive).
3. Named NPC **Houses** fill every empty seat, warm the seats friends hold, take over the trade of players who leave,
   and hand both to the human who comes next.
4. An absent friend's estate keeps running at 100% (it is an idle game). No reward ever counts how many humans
   took part. Nothing waits for anyone.
5. The station **Works** on the plaza is the shared project: stages over the season, ending in a sustained-flow
   finale that is pure pipe logistics. Each player is rewarded for their own share, and the perk is shared by
   everyone.

## 1 · The five weakest ideas (my own included), and how each one makes someone quit

### W1. Rewards that count humans: Quartet Recipes, relay goods, profession bonuses, and my own "diversity bonus"

*Sources:* 6-outsider #15 (Quartet items need four inputs from four different people; caretakers can't supply
them), 1-wild #13 (relay goods through three different companies) and #39 (profession bonuses only when trading
with a different profession), and my round-1 idea 7 (weekly crew reward ×1.0 / 1.2 / 1.35 / 1.5 by the number of
members who contributed).

**Failure scenario.** Ana, Ben and Cleo start on Monday; Dev's seat is held. Ben's job gets busy and he stops on
Wednesday (day 3). On Friday Ana opens the crew panel: "Quartet: 2 of 4 humans" and "This week's crew reward ×1.2
(Ben didn't contribute)". The number is honest, and that is the problem: it puts a price on Ben's absence. Cleo
writes in the group chat: "Ben, can you just log in and drop some coal, we lose 15%". Ben feels guilty, mutes the
chat, and never opens the game again, because coming back now means facing a debt. On day 10 Ana and Cleo talk
about "opening Ben's seat for someone active", which is a polite way of kicking him. Two weeks later the crew is
Ana and Dev, and the Quartet trophy will never happen. Ana stops in week 3, saying the game "punishes us because
Ben has a job".

**Why it fails:** it is the Habitica party-damage trap (5-lessons) and the Hay Day Derby trap: the absent player
causes the loss, so the group feels guilt and resentment. Egg, Inc. had the same problem with "sleepers" in co-ops.
**Replace with:** projects sized to the *active* residents, keystone credit for the delivery that completes a
missing good (4-economy M12), and personal rewards by share. A head count never appears in a formula or on the
screen.

### W2. My own round-1 Yard network: a shared mini-network for everyone in the Quad, with pipes anyone can re-route

*Source:* my round-1 ideas 5–6 (all crew members' Yard pipes join one network that trades at the crew price) plus
"residents of a Quad who aren't in a crew become a crew" in my migration, plus "Yard pipes can be re-routed by any
member, logged and undoable for 24 h".

**Failure scenario.** Sam plays alone and taps "Pick for us". He is placed in a Quad with Mo and Lin, two strangers,
and the migration rule makes the three of them a "Quad crew". Day 1: Sam's spare energy flows into the Yard and on
to Mo and Lin at the crew price; he never chose them, but it is fine. Day 4: Lin, an optimiser, re-routes the Yard
pipes so that his Steel Mill sits next to the energy inlet. The Yard network now serves Lin first, and Sam's
Glassworks, which used to get Mo's energy, runs at 60%. The log says "Lin moved 6 pipes". Sam undoes it; Lin redoes
it the next morning. Sam can't leave the Yard without losing the crew price and the Workshop perk. On day 6 he
writes "this is the old district problem in a smaller box" in the feedback form and quits. Meanwhile, in another
Quad, Ana and Ben have fallen out. Ben's coal is still part of "their" network, and the only way Ana can stop
trading with him is a per-resource switch that also stops her trading with Cleo.

**Why it fails:** it brings back the owner's original complaint (nobody chose a partner, others' flows run through
your pipes) at a smaller scale, and it creates a shared space where one person's change hurts another.
**Replace with:** a plaza where the game, not the players, draws the pipes, and where the junction is a
*switchboard of chosen pairs*, not a network.

### W3. Moving the map under people's feet: the accordion map and drifting islands

*Source:* 1-wild #7 (every night the map is re-packed so active estates slide together) and #5 (islands drift closer
when linked and drift away when abandoned).

**Failure scenario.** Day 9: Ana has spent two evenings on a 46-tile copper pipe through the wilderness to her
outpost, and she has a link through her west gate to Fox & Sons next door. Two estates near her go dormant. That
night the accordion re-packs the district: Ana's estate slides to where the dormant ones were, her west gate now
faces a different station, the game re-routes her link to Fox & Sons at three times the length (14% losses instead
of 3%), and her outpost pipe is re-laid along a path she never chose. Ben's resting estate, which she tended every
day, has been folded out of view into the "sleeping quarter". She opens the game to a map she doesn't recognise.
The place she picked on day 1, for its wind and its neighbours, is gone. Forge of Empires learned this with its
reshuffled neighbourhoods: you never bond with neighbours who keep changing.

**Why it fails:** in TransportPipes geography carries meaning (deposits, wind, rivers, pipe lengths, who lives
next to you). Moving it breaks choosing (checklist 2), clarity (9) and the pipe game itself (10).
**Replace with:** an estate moves only when its owner taps (or when it is asleep and nobody holds its seat).

### W4. Personal hub saturation that halves the price, on a quiet server

*Source:* 4-economy M7: the hub pays full price up to an hourly allowance `A = 600 g/h × Era × (1 + Level/10)` per
resource, then halves for each further A.

**Failure scenario.** The numbers don't fit the catalog. One Small Factory at level 1 makes 6 iron ingots a minute,
about 5,000 g/h at the hub, while A at Level 1 is 660 g/h. So the "day-1 player never sees it" claim is wrong by
almost 8×: every player sees it from their first factory. Even if A meant per minute, here is what happens on a
server of five. Sam, a Coal Valley specialist, builds his second Big Factory on day 9 and crosses the allowance.
The UI says "the hub is full of your iron ingots. Your station buys at 1.4, Anna's Steel Mill would pay 1.75". But
there is no station market with buyers and no Anna, only Houses with limited demand. His best product now earns
half, and the optimal play becomes "make a little of everything", which is the opposite of the specialisation the
whole trade design relies on. To Sam it feels like a hidden tax for growing (inequity aversion, 5-lessons A8). He
quits on day 11.

**Why it fails:** the goal is right (one player's dumping must not ruin everybody's price), but a steep personal
penalty turns "people are better" into "being alone is punished".
**Keep the goal, change the shape:** your sales move only your own price; it softens late (3 hours of a typical
same-Level company's output of that good) and gently; it never drops below 0.7; and Houses' demand grows with the
server.

### W5. Making absence cost your friends: the caretaker at 50–60% after two days, shift logs and night markets

*Sources:* 6-outsider #3 (after ~2 days offline your seat runs at 50–60%), #9 (Shift Log: a crew bonus for every
day that somebody "took the shift"), #29 (Night Market: one hour a day pays extra for goods delivered together);
1-wild #16 (a clocktower boost only while someone tended it in the last 8 hours).

**Failure scenario.** Cleo goes to her sister's wedding from Friday to Monday. On Saturday, day 2 away, the
caretaker takes her seat and her glass output drops from 300 to 165 a minute. Ana's Circuit line, which buys that
glass, runs at half speed for two days. On Sunday nobody "takes the shift", the clocktower stops ringing and the
crew's coverage run ends at 11 days. On Monday Cleo finds three chat messages ("are you around?", "the tower
stopped", "np, enjoy!") and a lost bonus. The next weekend she opens the game from a dinner table at 23:55 just to
"tend the tower". By week 3 she says it feels like a job, and she leaves.

**Why it fails:** in an idle game an absent player's buildings run anyway, so cutting their output is a punishment
the game invents. Shift coverage and timed markets are a "both online" requirement in disguise.
**Replace with:** the estate runs at 100% while its owner is away; the caretaker only makes *decisions* (renewing
contracts, selling overflow); no group streaks; no time-of-day rewards.

## 2 · The five strongest ideas, and what makes each one strong

### S1. Estates are islands; trade is a bilateral link with a tank and a hub fallback

*Sources:* TradingRethink option A, 4-economy M5 + M11 (auto-routed, non-exclusive corridor links; contract tanks;
the hub fallback), 1-wild #21 + #14 ("lines, not webs", gate tanks).

It removes the root cause (transitive merging of networks) and keeps the pipe as the act of partnership. Every trade
names two companies. A link can't block anyone, because corridors hold link bundles, not owned tiles. A partner
is upside only, because a tank holds 2 hours of flow and the hub fills in when the partner stops. The simulation stays
small (teleporter-style links, which already exist). Every other good idea depends on this one.

### S2. Honest link states and successor Houses that inherit the trade

*Sources:* 3-lonely §3.1–3.2; my round-1 idea 11 ("a Lodger who copies Ben's last 7 days of trade").

One cheap mechanism (tenants already exist) covers leavers (4), the lonely (3) and async play (7). The gap is filled
before it opens, and every zero on a pipe has a sentence explaining it ("Dara's estate sailed to the Quiet Isles;
Hollow & Sons keep buying your coal"). Its best side effect is that a newcomer who takes the seat later inherits a
proven partner who is already waiting.

### S3. The invite link that holds a real seat, warmed by a named House

*Sources:* 5-lessons #2, 1-wild #1 + #44, my round-1 ideas 1–2, 9–10.

"We live together" is the first line of a friends' story, and this makes it exact (Travian's quadrants were only
rough). The friend who joins a week later has a seat, a role and the House's trades waiting for them. No stranger can
take the seat. While the seat waits, the House in it produces and trades, so the wait costs the friends nothing.

### S4. Tending: one tap, only positive, banked, and counted for the return

*Sources:* 3-lonely §2.3 ("Ana tended your estate 17 times"), 5-lessons #5 (favours that bank up for 3 days).

It costs almost nothing to build (a counter and an 8-hour buff), needs nobody online, and gives a daily reason to
visit. A player whose friends have stopped still plays "with" them. It also gives the game's strongest win-back line,
"someone missed you", without guilt, because the tending is counted, never asked for. Banking is essential: it keeps
tending from becoming Forge of Empires' daily motivate chore.

### S5. Station Works: basket stages, a sustained-flow finale, keystone credit, and per-share rewards for a shared perk

*Sources:* 4-economy M12–M13, 5-lessons #9.

The late-game goal is logistics: a sustained flow needs pipe throughput, buffers and timing, which are the game's
core skills. Specialisation is celebrated through keystone credit, not required. A non-contributor costs nobody
anything, because the perk is a public good and personal rewards follow your own share. The size scales with the
active residents, so a solo station finishes. It leaves a monument on the map with names on it.

Close runners-up: prices as a position in the fair band with three presets (4-economy M3: no stale prices, bounded
funnelling); presence without simultaneity, meaning lights on, usual hours and change sparkles (3-lonely §1.3); the
return in three screens with landing cards and a savings jar (3-lonely §4); coloured pellets per owner (6, 1, mine);
leagues of about 25 players at the same stage (5-lessons #10).

## 3 · Six files chose a small group around a shared junction. Right, or groupthink?

**Partly groupthink.** The brief itself says "choosing a station / position would be ideal", and all six files
picked up the word. But the shape also stands up on its own merits:

- **Dunbar's first layer is about 5.** Three friends plus one or two people they come to know is the warm layer;
  a district of 24 is the "people I recognise" layer and is already too big to bond with (5-lessons A2).
- **A shared object to plug into.** On a grid, two of four friends are diagonal and share no edge. A plaza at the
  inner crossing is the only place where all four meet, and it is where the shared project can stand.
- **It bounds the simulation.** A station is a handful of estates, solved on its own.
- **Choosing a table is a small joy** (Catan's first placement) if the information is good.

**Where the convergent version breaks for friends:**

| Case | What breaks if station = crew = market = project | Fix in the revised design |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A couple (2)** | They hold two empty seats for weeks or share "their" crew and network with two strangers | **Halts**: 2-seat stations on the arms of the district (4 per district); couples get a halt by default |
| **Three friends** | Fine: three seats plus one held | The held seat is warmed by a House; after the hold, the crew decides whether to keep the House or open the seat |
| **Six friends** | Don't fit in 4; the 5th and 6th live next door as "neighbours", second-class for the project and the friend price | **A crew can span a station and the neighbouring halts** (up to 8); a short crew pipe joins the plazas; crew links work across it |
| **Friends who fall out** | You can't leave the shared network without leaving your home; the other one's goods keep flowing through "your" Yard | Leave the crew in one tap; links pause after 1 h notice; nobody can evict anybody; a free move once per season |
| **Strangers placed in your station** | They inherit your friend price, chat and project; their flows mix with yours | Seats a crew holds are never given to strangers; strangers are **neighbours**: a shared plaza and Works, trade only through links both sides chose |
| **One human in a 4-seat station** | No market at all; economy's answer (an anonymous pooled station market) brings back "everyone trades with everyone" in a smaller box | Houses in the other seats, link cards that are one tap to accept, the hub as the always-there bank |
| **The group outgrows its place** | Six friends want to move together | Crew blocks (corporation blocks already exist, 72 h): the crew reserves a free station + halt and moves as one |

**Verdict:** the shape is right but it carries too much. A station is a *place*. A crew is *people* and can cross
station borders. A link is a *trade relationship* that both sides chose. The plaza is a switchboard of pairs and a
building site, not a network.

## 4 · The revised design: stations, crews and links

### 4.1 The layers at a glance

| Layer | What it is | Size | Who decides | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Station** | A place: seats around a plaza | 4 seats (station) or 2 (halt) | You pick a seat | Neighbours, the junction, the Works, the notice board |
| **Crew** | People: friends with a name and banner | 1–8, from Level 1 | Invite link; leave in one tap | Held seats, pre-approved links, colours, tending, the crew board, the postcard, heirlooms |
| **Link** | A trade: one pipe, two companies | Pairs | Both sides (Houses accept automatically) | Goods at a band price, tank, fallback, honest state |
| **House ⚙** | A named NPC company, always labelled | Fills seats | The game | Warms held seats, fills empty ones, inherits leavers' trade, hands over to humans |
| **District** | Hub + 4 stations + 4 halts | 24 seats | Opens on demand | Hub market, district Wonder, leagues, the Line Race |
| **Corporation** | Unchanged (Level 8+, up to 30) | Many crews | Existing | Big goals; a crew keeps its banner inside a corp |

### 4.2 The map: today's 5×5 district, read as stations

  row 1 │ N1       │ N1       │ h1       │ N2       │ N2       │

  row 2 │ h2       ◎ h2       │   HUB    │ h3       ◎ h3       │
        ├──────────┼──────────┼──────────┼──────────┼──────────┤
  row 3 │ N3       │ N3       │ h4       │ N4       │ N4       │
        ├──────────◉──────────┼────◎─────┼──────────◉──────────┤

  4 stations × 4 + 4 halts × 2 = 24 seats, the same as today

One station, zoomed in:

```
   ┌──────────────────┬──────────────────┐
   │ Ana              │              Ben │
   │ Windy Heights    │      Coal Valley │
   │          ┌───────┼───────┐          │   Each estate gives its inner-corner parcel (8×8) to the
   │          │ Ana's │ Ben's │          │   plaza and gets a fifth gate, the junction gate, where
   ├──────────┼──── WORKS ────┼──────────┤   its start parcels meet the plaza. Each owner may
   │          │Cleo's │ Dev's │          │   decorate their own quarter (cosmetic only).
   │          └───────┼───────┘          │   The game draws the junction pipes; nobody else
   │ Cleo             │     Copper Gulch │   can lay or remove pipes on the plaza.
   │ Riverside        │    (held: Dev)   │
   └──────────────────┴──────────────────┘
```

- **The parcel cost is small:** the inner-corner parcel is one of the last a player buys (parcels 15–16 come at Level
  45–60), so almost nobody has built there. Each estate keeps 15 parcels.
- **Seats are generated to complement each other:** every station has one energy seat (Windy Heights or Sunny
  Flats), one ore seat (Copper Gulch or Coal Valley), one water/sand seat (Riverside) and one materials seat (Old
  Forest or Stone Quarry). Every seat also has small ★2 deposits of every Era 1 basic (4-economy M1), so nobody is
  ever stuck. Halts get two complementary seats.
- **Districts open on demand**, so land is never scarce: when the newest district has fewer than two free stations,
  the next one opens.

### 4.3 Choosing: the station board and the default

The start screen has **Start alone** and **Start with friends**. Both lead to the **station board**, which is the
district map zoomed out to stations, with one card per station or halt:

| On the card | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Free seats and their land | "2 free: Riverside (sand ★5, river) · Copper Gulch (copper ★5)" |
| Residents | "Mo ⓛ14 · lights on 6 of 7 days · usually here: evenings" / "Bertha's Copperworks ⚙ (House)" |
| What they make and need | "Sells: energy, coal · Looking for: glass" |
| The Works | "Water Tower finished · Workshop 40%" |
| Neighbours | The neighbouring stations' names, banners and lights |
| The district | Hub demand this week, hub stage (Village → Metropolis), "busiest in the evening" |
| Hosts | A lantern when a resident welcomes newcomers (3-lonely §5.2) |

- **"Pick for us"** (the default, one tap) scores seats: +3 per active neighbour within 10 Levels or a Host, +2 for a
  waiting link at the seat (inherited from a House), +2 for similar usual hours, +1 per House neighbour, −3 when the
  neighbours are only resting ones (3-lonely §5.1). With friends it picks a station with enough free seats. A couple
  gets a halt.
- **Moving is free in your first 3 days** and once per season to join a crew, then weekly as now (relocation,
  crates and swaps exist).

### 4.4 Crews

- **Founding takes 15 seconds:** a name, one of 40 preset emblems (no uploads, nothing to moderate) and two colours.
  The founder gets one **seat invite per held seat** (a code tied to that seat) plus a general crew link that
  joins the crew but needs a founder's tap when it is used after 24 hours. A link that gets posted publicly can't
  give a stranger Dev's seat.
- **Held seats:** 14 days, renewable once (28 days in all) while the crew has an active member. A House warms the
  seat meanwhile at a capped size (about 30% of an active player's trade by week 2).
- **What crew members get:** pre-approved links with each other (no requests), the crew's colours on roofs, gate
  flags and pipe stripes, coloured pellets in shared pipes, the crew ticker, the crew board (Wishes), tending, the
  weekly crew postcard, and heirlooms that carry over to the next season. **No shared wallet, no crew levels, no
  crew-only rewards that count members.**
- **Size and shape:** 1–8, normally one station; a crew may also take the neighbouring halts. A **crew pipe** (a
  short game-drawn link between two plazas, 16–20 tiles) carries crew links across.
- **Leaving and removal:** leave in one tap (your links stay as normal links, which you can pause). A majority of
  the *active* members can remove someone from the crew; that person keeps their seat and estate and becomes a
  neighbour. Nobody can be evicted from a seat.
- **Sitter (only by the owner's consent, set in advance):** "If I'm away 7+ days, Ana may change my trade settings
  and storage shares." She can never build, demolish, move or spend gold. This is Travian's sitter in its harmless
  form.

### 4.5 Links and prices: one ladder, one sentence

A **price is a position in the fair band** between the hub's buy price (1.0×) and its sell price (2.5×): price =
1 + 1.5 × position. It moves with the hub by itself, so nobody's prices go stale.

| Price | Position | × hub base | Who |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hub buys | 0% | 1.0 | Always, your Trading Post |
| Mates' rate | 25% | 1.375 | Optional, to be generous with friends |
| Houses | 40% | 1.6 | NPC Houses, volume-limited |
| **Auto (default, also for crews)** | 50% | 1.75 | Everyone who doesn't care |
| Premium | 75% | 2.125 | Traders |
| Hub sells | 100% | 2.5 | Imports |

*"Neighbours pay best, Houses nearly as much, the hub least."*

- **Inside a station** every estate has one **junction pipe** from its junction gate into the plaza's manifold. The
  manifold carries **pair links**: crew pairs automatically, others when both sides accept (Houses always accept).
  The loss is the plaza distance only (~10 tiles, about 3% on grey pipes). The capacity is your junction pipe's
  tier, shared by your pair links: grey 100/min, copper 500, steel 5,000. **The first pipe decision of the game is
  upgrading your junction pipe** when your coal or glass outgrows it, usually on day 2–3.
- **Across stations:** gate-to-gate links, auto-routed through the corridors as bundles; no corridor tile can be
  owned, so nobody can block one (4-economy M5).
- **Every link** has a **tank** at the buyer's gate (2 hours of its flow), a **1-hour notice** for pauses and price
  changes, and an automatic **fallback** (a House, then hub imports for that input only, up to the lost amount). Its
  state is always shown in one sentence (*Live · Partner resting · Caretaker · Handed over · Capped*, 3-lonely §3.1).
- **Fair-price band for newcomers:** between companies 10+ Levels apart, the lower one always sells at ≥ hub price
  and buys at ≤ hub price +5%. Nobody below Level 6 can sign contracts with a shortfall clause.
- **The hub:** your sales move only *your* price for that good. The softening starts at 3 hours of a typical
  same-Level company's output, falls gently and never drops below 0.7. Tier 3+ goods have a daily import allowance
  at 2.5× (then 4.0×) so specialists matter in Era 2+ (4-economy M8), but every chain can still be built at home.
- **The solo-parity target:** with Houses at 1.6× and limited volume, a solo player trades about 20% of their output,
  a crew player about 35% at 1.75×. Solo earns about 0.2 × 1.6 + 0.8 × 1.0 = 1.12 against the crew's 0.35 × 1.75 +
  0.65 × 1.0 = 1.26, so about **89%**. Friends are clearly better, and solo is never a reason to quit. The House
  volume is the knob.

### 4.6 The plaza and the Works

The Works is the station's project for the season, on the plaza, open to every resident (crew or not) and to Houses.

| Stage | When (typical) | Basket (example for a 4-seat station, 3 active) | Unlocks for everyone in the station |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 · Foundation | First session (Level 3) | 300 ⚡ + 60 iron ingots: one player alone takes about 15 min | The plaza lights, the notice board |
| 1 · Water Tower | Day 1–2 | Bricks, planks, water | +5% water and energy |
| 2 · Workshop | Day 4–7 | Iron ingots, copper ingots, glass | +5% production of one chosen chain (vote of active residents) |
| 3 · Foundry Hall | Week 2 | Steel, concrete, bricks | Junction capacity +50% |
| Finale · First Blast | Week 3–4 | **Sustained flow: 600 steel/min for 6 hours**, from the station's tanks and pipes | The station's Landmark on the district map, the chapter perk, the heirloom |

- **The size follows the active residents** (played in the last 3 days); Houses count as 0.5 and deliver slowly by
  themselves, so a station with one human finishes (later, but it finishes). When someone rests mid-stage, the
  remainder shrinks.
- **Credit:** your own share sets your personal reward (gold, XP, your colour in the banners, your name on the
  plaque). **Keystone:** the delivery that completes a stage's last missing good gets +20% credit and a ticker line
  ("Noor's glass completed the Foundry Hall!"). Hub imports count only 25%. Shares are shown as a ring of colours,
  **never as a ranking and never as a list of who didn't deliver.**
- **The finale is a pipe puzzle, not an appointment.** The 6-hour window opens by itself when the Works' tank holds
  1 hour of steel, and it *pauses* (never fails) if the flow drops. Friends fill tanks on their own schedules, and
  the junction pipes and their upgrades decide whether it can run.

### 4.7 The async kit (nothing needs two people online)

| Tool | What happens | Guardrail |
| --- | --- | --- |

### 4.9 Arrivals and returns

- **The late friend takes the held seat from its House.** The House leaves a **starter kit**: 3–5 of its buildings on
  the start parcels at level ≤ 5 (its other buildings leave with it), plus **its links**, which now carry the
  friend's name. A short tutorial still teaches the first steps on top of the kit.
- **Tailwind (late-start XP):** XP × (1 + days since the season started / 14), at most ×2.5, until the player is 5
  Levels below the median of the active players around them (crew, or station if solo) (3-lonely §5.4). Tech
  diffusion as now.
- **Housewarming crate:** each crew mate may send one crate in the newcomer's first 48 h, worth at most 1 hour of
  the giver's own production of one good, and only to players below Level 10. That is enough to feel welcome and
  too little to funnel gold.
- **The return in three screens** (3-lonely §4.1): one *while you were away* screen with 5 items and a 10-second
  timelapse, then "your estate is safe", then *where do you want to be?* (it is still in its held seat, or landing
  cards: next to your crew / your old spot / a lively spot / the newest district). Landing is free and takes a
  minute. Then the **Era jump kit** for every Era missed (a blueprint of the standard chain, a free crate of its
  first building, its first 3 techs at −75%) and a "Back!" ribbon for 7 days.

### 4.10 Late game and friendly competition

- **The Works finale** (4.6) for every station.
- **Trunk lines** (4-economy M9, simplified): built by a consortium (a crew, a station or a corporation) along
  corridors and through the wilderness, in **10-tile segments that keep their builder's colour and name**, with a
  Pump House every 25 tiles that a member can adopt (an unadopted one works at base level). Capacity comes in 20
  slots; each slot is a bilateral link. Spare slots can be rented at a toll of at most 3%, with 24 h notice. When
  every owner has been dormant for 14 days, the hub buys the line at 50% and runs it as a public line.
- **District Line Race:** at a Discovery (a rich wilderness deposit, announced 6 h ahead), the first three stations
  to pipe their plaza to it get a permanent marker on the map and a trophy on the plaza.
- **Leagues of about 25 companies at the same stage**, re-grouped weekly with no demotion. **Station Cup** per active
  resident, your best 3 days of the week count. **Mastery titles** per resource ("Master Glassmaker of Ashford").
- **Seasons:** crews persist. The station's Landmark becomes an heirloom (a small copy stands on next season's plaza).
  At the next world the crew settles again through the same lobby, with held seats for absent members.

| 5 | **Away and back:** estates run at 100%, held seats instead of the Quiet Isles, tending, the savings jar, autopilot reinvest, the sitter, the return in three screens, jump kits, Tailwind, housewarming | Activity stages, caretaker, Quiet Isles, relocation | 2 |

| 7 | **Late game:** trunk lines with slots, rents and hub takeover; the Line Race; leagues by stage; the Station Cup; mastery; own-price hub softening | Outposts, Discovery events, leaderboards | 2–3 |

Steps 1–3 already make "three friends tonight" work; 4–5 protect the lonely and the leavers; 6–7 are week 2 and
later.

**Migration for the testers' worlds:**
- Every district keeps its 5×5 layout: corner groups become stations, arm pairs become halts. Nobody moves.
- Buildings on an inner-corner parcel (rare, since it is bought last) are packed into crates with a full refund
  option, and the owner gets the parcel's price back.
- Corridor pipes become links as in TradingRethink (gate-to-gate chains become links, the rest is refunded).
- Tenants become Houses and keep their trades.
- No one is put in a crew automatically; each player gets one card, "You traded most with X and Y. Form a crew?"
- Corporations stay as they are.

## 8 · Risks that remain

1. **Houses are the backbone of solo play and of absence, and their quality decides a lot.** If their writing is
   flat, solo feels like playing with vending machines. If they are too generous, friends feel optional. The knobs
   are the 1.6× price, the volume cap at about 30% of an active player, and personality (letters, requests,
   plaques). This needs a balance run and real testers.
2. **Three concepts to teach** (station, crew, link). The order helps: station on minute 1, crew only if you
   started with friends, links appear as one-tap cards from Level 3. But the station board and the crew lobby must
   be very clear.
3. **Held seats can make stations look closed.** A station with two held seats and two Houses looks full to
   strangers. That is fine while districts open on demand, but on a small server it could hide the only lively
   station. Mitigation: held seats count as "warm" on the board, and the 28-day maximum.
4. **The plaza takes a parcel from every estate.** Few testers have built there, but the art (plaza tiles, the Works
   stages, the Landmark) is new work, and it has to sit exactly on its footprint like every building.
5. **The finale's sustained flow could favour optimisers.** Cleo will love it, Ben won't care. That's acceptable
   because it pauses instead of failing and rewards each player's own share, but it needs a clear "what's missing"
   line on the Works panel so non-optimisers can help without a spreadsheet.
6. **Crews spanning a station and halts** need careful UI: which plaza is "ours", where the crew pipe runs. If it
   is confusing, cap crews at 6 (a station plus one halt) for v1.
