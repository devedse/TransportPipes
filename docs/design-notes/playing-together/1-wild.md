# Wild ideas: how players play together in TransportPipes

Role: wild idea thrower. Quantity and boldness over polish. Numbers in brackets = checklist points from the brief
(1 friends · 2 choosing · 3 lonely · 4 stopping · 5 early · 6 late · 7 async · 8 fair · 9 clear · 10 pipes ·
11 scale · 12 seasons · 13 social · 14 buildable).

---

## A. Positions & maps

### 1. Hamlets: four seats around a well
Throw away the 5×5 district. The atom of the world is a **hamlet**: 4 estates around a shared courtyard (the "well")
with one common building. You pick a hamlet the way you pick a table in a pub: the map shows seats taken, the
owners' banners, how recently they played (a little lamp: lit / dim / dark) and what the ground holds. Friends
reserve the other 3 seats with an invite code; a seat stays reserved for 14 days. [1, 2, 5, 9, 13, 14]

### 2. Station picker on a rail map
The world is a rail map (a metro diagram, not terrain). Every **station** has 3–6 estate slots; lines connect
stations. Joining = clicking a station on the diagram, which shows its residents, activity, specialty ("ore-rich",
"riverside", "near the capital") and free slots. Default button: "put me where people are active and there's room
for 3 more". Neighbours are the station, not whoever happened to register before you. [1, 2, 9, 11, 13]

### 3. Estates on rails: your estate IS a train car
Wildest version of the station idea: each estate is a long carriage. Friends **couple** their carriages into one
train; pipes run car-to-car through the couplings. The train moves slowly around a world loop, stopping at hubs
(different hubs buy different goods, so the trip matters). A lone player is a locomotive with one car who can couple
to any train that has a free coupling. A player who stops is **uncoupled at a siding** and becomes an NPC depot car
that other trains can pick up. [1, 3, 4, 10, 13]

### 4. River rafts that moor together
Estates are rafts on a slow, wide river. You **moor** to a dock next to friends (dock = station with 4 berths);
unmooring lets you drift to another dock in a few hours. Inactive rafts slowly drift downstream to the estuary (an
archive harbour) and the berth frees up; a returning player sails back up. Moving is natural, no hole is ever left.
[1, 2, 4, 11, 12]

### 5. Sky islands with bridge-pipes
Each estate is a floating island. Pipes are literally **bridges** between islands, and islands that stay connected
for a week drift closer together (shorter, cheaper pipes). Disconnected, abandoned islands drift away into the
clouds. The map is a living picture of who plays with whom. [1, 4, 9, 10, 13]

### 6. The moving van (relocate any time)
Your estate can be **packed into a crate** (all buildings stored as a blueprint, parcels kept) and unpacked on a
free spot elsewhere. Once a week it's free, otherwise it costs a day's income. Wrong neighbours, a dead hamlet, a
friend who joined late: just move next to them. Blueprints already exist (v1.1), so this is the selection blueprint
applied to the whole estate. [1, 2, 4, 6, 14]

### 7. The accordion map
The world layout is **logical, not physical**: every season (or every night) the map is re-packed so active
estates slide together and dormant ones are folded out of view into a "sleeping quarter". Corridors and pipes are
re-routed automatically between hamlet members. The map is never full of dead estates. [4, 11, 12, 9]

### 8. Watershed: upstream and downstream
The map is a river basin. Upstream spots have raw resources (ore, water, timber); downstream spots near the sea
hub have cheap shipping and factories. Pipes flowing downhill are cheap, uphill pipes need pumps. Choosing a spot is
choosing a role, and an upstream miner and a downstream smelter are natural partners. [2, 6, 10, 9]

### 9. Land rush at season start
Every season opens with a **land rush**: a fresh map, a 24-hour window, everyone plants flags. Groups plant
together ("we take this hamlet"). Late joiners pick from what remains or from the expansion ring. Old estates migrate
as a crate (idea 6) into the new map at the owner's chosen spot. [1, 2, 12, 13]

### 10. Frontier rings
The map grows outward in rings. The centre is the old, busy, dense core (veterans, capital, expensive land); the
outer ring is fresh frontier where newcomers and new groups settle with cheap land and their own small hub. A group
can "found" a new station on the frontier. Newcomers never land in a veteran's shadow. [2, 5, 6, 8, 11]

### 11. Twin estates
Two friends can **merge** their estates into a compound: shared parcels, one bigger factory floor, both names on the
gate, earnings split by who owns which building. If one leaves, the compound splits back cleanly along the original
line. [1, 4, 6, 13]

## B. Cooperation mechanics

### 12. The Well (hamlet commons machine)
Each hamlet's courtyard holds a shared machine every member can pipe into: e.g. a **Foundry** that needs coal from
one, ore from another, water from a third and pays all of them a bonus product none can make alone. Inputs bank in
the well's tanks, so it runs while people sleep; an empty input is filled at a premium by the NPC market so nobody
stalls. [1, 6, 7, 10, 13]

### 13. Relay recipes (baton goods)
Special high-value goods must pass through **three different companies** in sequence (ore → ingot at A, ingot →
gear at B, gear → clockwork at C). Each step buffers in a gate tank, so it works fully async. The good carries a tag
with all three company colours and sells at a big premium; every handler gets a cut. [1, 6, 7, 10, 13]

### 14. Standing orders with gate tanks
Every gate has a **tank**. Trade between estates is "fill my tank up to X at price P"; the partner's pipe fills it
whenever their production runs. Nobody needs to be online; the tank shows on the map how full it is and who feeds
it. If the partner stops, the NPC market tops it up at a slightly worse price. [7, 9, 10, 4, 8]

### 15. The steward (NPC proxy)
When a player has been offline for 3 days, an NPC **steward** (a little character with a clipboard) takes over:
keeps their machines running at reduced speed, honours their contracts, sells the surplus. Partners keep their
supply; the returning player finds a short report ("the steward sold 2,000 coal while you were away"). [4, 7, 3]

### 16. Shift handover
For friends in different time zones: a hamlet building (the **clocktower**) gives a boost while someone in the
hamlet has "tended" it in the last 8 hours. Three friends spread across the day keep it ringing 24/7. A shared
logbook shows who tended when. Cooperation becomes "cover each other's shift". [1, 7, 13]

### 17. Sharecropping
Lend an unused parcel to a neighbour: they build on it, the output is split (you choose 20–50 %). Lonely or
part-time players earn from land they can't use yet; busy players get more room. Leases end automatically if the
tenant stops. [1, 3, 4, 6, 8]

### 18. Apprentice and master
A veteran can **take an apprentice** (a newcomer in the same station). The master gets a slice of the apprentice's
XP; the apprentice gets a one-way "gift pipe" from the master and a mentor quest line. Gifts are capped by the
apprentice's level, so it can't funnel gold to alts. [5, 6, 8, 13]

### 19. Seed colonies
A veteran can **found an estate for a newcomer** next to their own (pay the land). The colony starts with a
starter pipe and a few buildings. The founder earns a small share while the colony lives; the newcomer owns it fully.
A reason to invite friends, and a reason for veterans to welcome strangers. [1, 5, 6, 13]

### 20. Bounty pins
Post a request on the map: "need 200 coal/h at my east gate, paying 12 g". It shows as a glowing pin. Anyone who
pipes into the gate tank collects. Newcomers get an obvious first cooperation goal; lonely players find partners on
the map instead of in a chat. [3, 5, 9, 10, 13]

## C. Pipes & logistics

### 21. Lines, not webs
An inter-estate pipe is a **line** with exactly two ends and a visible manifest ("coal → Mira, 40/h, 11 g").
Lines never merge into a network; a third partner needs a third line (bundled lines share a trench visually). Who
trades with whom is literally the set of lines on the map. Ends district-wide mega-networks for good. [9, 10, 11, 8]

### 22. Trunk lines (crowdfunded pipe highways)
Big pipes between stations are **trunks**: a group project. Anyone can fund a segment and earns a toll on every unit
that passes it. A trunk is a carrier with on-ramps (stations), not a merged network: goods enter at A with an
address and leave at B. Building the trunk to the next station is the mid-game goal of a station. [6, 10, 11, 13]

### 23. Pneumatic post
Small capsules between any two estates in the world, flying through a tube network that runs above the map
(visible, animated). Send gifts, samples, rare parts, or a "welcome basket". Capsules are small, so they can't
replace pipes, but they connect anyone with anyone without merging networks. [3, 9, 10, 13]

### 24. The Great Pipe (world spine)
One single server-wide pipeline that the whole world extends together each season, from the capital outwards. Each
station can build an on-ramp. The spine's length is the world's shared score, and the season's wonder sits at its
end. "Transcontinental railroad" moment. [6, 10, 12, 13]

### 25. Pressure and pumps
Long pipes lose pressure; **pump stations** restore it. A long line to a friend two stations away needs pumps built
by players along the route, who earn a fee. Distance becomes a cooperative engineering problem instead of a silent
loss. [6, 10, 8]

### 26. The shared river (commons with decay)
A river runs through every station. Anyone upstream can dump goods into it; anyone downstream can fish them out.
Goods decay as they float. It's chaotic, generous and very readable (you see crates floating by). Quotas prevent
flooding; dumping earns "generosity" reputation. [3, 10, 13, 9]

### 27. Underground layer
Pipes between non-adjacent estates run in a **deep layer** below the map (a toggleable view), so they never cut
through someone else's land or gates. Mines connect to shared ore veins under several estates. Blocking becomes
impossible. [8, 10, 9]

### 28. Pipe colours and stripes
Your pipes have your company colour; a line between two companies is striped in both. A station's pipe pattern is
its fingerprint on the map. Cheap, huge for clarity and identity. [9, 13, 14]

## D. Churn & loneliness

### 29. Time capsule
After 2 weeks of absence (and the steward, idea 15), the estate is **packed into a capsule**: buildings, storage,
level, kept in full. The land is freed. A returning player opens the capsule anywhere they like, e.g. next to the
friends who are still playing. Nobody blocks land, nobody loses progress. [4, 12, 1]

### 30. Adoption
An abandoned estate can be **adopted** by a hamlet neighbour as a temporary annex. The adopter runs it and keeps a
share; if the original owner returns, they get it back with a "while you were away" bonus. Neighbours inherit a
place, not a hole. [4, 6, 8]

### 31. NPC neighbours with personalities
Empty seats in a hamlet are filled by named NPC families (the Millers, a grumpy smith, a tea merchant) who send
requests, gifts and small quests and trade by pipe like a player. When a real player joins, the NPC "moves out"
with a farewell gift. A lonely player always has a full, lively hamlet. [3, 4, 5, 13]

### 32. Echo trade
On quiet servers, the hub generates demand from **echoes** of real players' past behaviour (anonymised patterns from
other worlds and earlier seasons): "a smelter two stations away wants coal". Lonely players feel a living economy
that is shaped by real play. [3, 11]

### 33. Message in a bottle
Throw a capsule (gift + note) into the void; it lands with a random active player anywhere. They can reply or send
something back. A gentle, safe social channel for loners and the shy. [3, 13]

### 34. Welcome wagon
A newcomer's arrival is an event: neighbours get a quest "pipe a welcome basket (5 goods) to the newcomer",
rewarding both. The newcomer immediately sees friendly pipes arriving at their gates. [5, 13, 3]

### 35. Ghost lanterns
Players who stop leave a **lantern** on their (packed) spot, with their banner and best stat. Hamlets keep a small
memorial wall of past members. The world remembers people; returning players see their lantern still lit. [4, 13]

## E. Late game

### 36. Guild city
A corporation can found its own **city** (a new hub) on the frontier: it sets its hub's buy list and prices, other
players can settle around it, and it grows (districts, statues, a skyline) with deliveries. The ultimate group
project, and it creates new stations for newcomers. [6, 13, 2, 11]

### 37. Station league
Each week every station gets the same delivery target; stations race on a league table, with banners on the map
for the winners. Friendly competition between groups, cooperation within. Small stations are scored per member.
[6, 13, 8]

### 38. World events
"The Drought" (water scarce; piped stations share), "The Comet" (a rare ore crater appears in the wilderness; groups
race to pipe to it), "The Fair" (the capital buys luxury goods at double price for 48 h). A reason to log in and to
coordinate. [6, 13, 12]

### 39. Professions that need each other
At level 10 you choose a profession (Miner, Smelter, Engineer, Merchant). Each gets bonuses only when trading with a
**different** profession. A station of four friends naturally specialises; a lone player can still do all of it
at base speed. [6, 1, 3]

### 40. The Crown's contract
Huge timed orders ("10,000 steel beams in 5 days") that only a station or corp can accept together. Contributions
are tracked per member and shown on a big progress bar at the station. [6, 7, 13]

### 41. Wonder relay
The season's wonder needs parts produced at different stations and **physically piped** to the wonder site along
trunk lines. The wonder's construction shows the whole world's pipe network converging on one spot. [6, 10, 12, 13]

### 42. Patron of the frontier
Veterans can sponsor a new frontier station (pay for its hub). Newcomers who settle there get cheap land; the patron
earns reputation, a statue and a small toll, never control. Veterans help newcomers instead of crushing them. [5,
6, 8, 13]

## F. Social

### 43. Banners on the map
Every group has a name, colour and banner flying over its hamlet/station. Zoomed out, the map is a patchwork of
coloured territories. [9, 13, 14]

### 44. Invite = seat
An invite link is a **reserved seat** next to you, with a housewarming bonus for both when the friend's first pipe
reaches your gate. Joining a week later still works because the seat is held (or a moving van brings you next to
them). [1, 13]

### 45. Notice board
A physical notice board building at each station: async posts, offers, thank-yous, the station's weekly goal vote.
Communication without real-time chat. [7, 13, 9]

### 46. Station postcards
A one-click picture of your hamlet, framed with all members' banners, shareable outside the game, with an invite
seat attached. The cosy art becomes the invitation. [13, 1]

### 47. Town meeting vote
Each week station members vote on the station's goal (which good the commons machine makes, which trunk to build
next). Absent members' votes don't count, so the active ones steer. [6, 7, 13]

## My 5 favourites

1. **Hamlets: four seats around a well (1 + 12).** The most direct answer to "fun with 3 friends that join": the
   unit of the world is a table for four, you pick a table with full information, friends reserve seats, and the
   well gives the four of you something to pipe into together from minute one. Cheap to build on the current block
   grid.
2. **Lines, not webs + gate tanks (21 + 14).** Fixes the root cause of the mega-network: a pipe between companies
   is a visible two-ended line with a manifest and a tank at the gate. Clear on the map, async by nature, no third
   party in the middle, and it keeps pipes as the heart of cooperation.
3. **Steward → time capsule → moving van (15 + 29 + 6).** One chain that solves churn completely: a sleeping player
   keeps partners supplied, then packs away and frees the land, and comes back next to whoever they want. The same
   crate solves "friend joins a week later" and season migration.
4. **Estates on rails (3).** The wildest one and maybe the most "TransportPipes": your group literally is a train,
   pipes run through the couplings, the trip between hubs is the game's long logistics, and leaving players are
   just uncoupled at a siding. Even if it's too much, it points at the right feeling: a group is a vehicle you
   choose to join.
5. **Trunk lines and guild cities (22 + 36).** The late game: stations crowdfund trunk pipes to each other (a carrier
   with addresses, not a merged network), and corps found their own city on the frontier, which creates new stations
   for newcomers. Growth by expansion instead of rebuilding.
