# Round C · outsider: the exam for the first evening, the phone and fun

I examined the three specs (c-economy, c-friends for answer (a), c-wild for answer (b)) against run 1's evidence about
what players read, tap and skip. **The assumption:** the fix plan's F1–F5, F7, F8 and F10 ship underneath, as the
synthesis says. **The rule, as in round 4:** only what a spec says counts.

## The six tests

| # | Test | Pass if |
| --- | --- | --- |
| E1 | **The first 20 minutes** | Every friend has their own income, and by minute 20 sees one of their goods reach something shared or a friend, with nobody online together |
| E2 | **Ben, a skimmer on a phone** | His social game needs ≤ 3 single taps, none a drag. Each of his first three sessions ends with one line saying what his goods did |
| E3 | **Words and the first screen** | ≤ 5 new nouns and ≤ 2 social verbs on the first evening. The first screen shows one toast at a time plus Loady |
| E4 | **Send is real, and predictable** | The button changes where goods go, and a player can tell when it will be there |
| E5 | **Fun, not a chore** | No daily duty, no "unanswered", no line that blames a friend. The players who read (Cleo, Dev) can *feel* a gift; the skimmer gets relief without a tap |
| E6 | **No wall without a next step** | Every "you can't" names where the good comes from |

The tests rest on run 1: Ben opened a building panel only when Loady's *Show me* did (ben d01-2000 #15, d36-2000
#81–#84), everyone clicked through the return dialogs (23 "Collect and continue" clicks), and Ben's first phone screen
was a pile (ben d01-2000-0014).

**The verbs pass.** c-friends deliberately holds the line: Send and Thanks, with Sketch only when a friend is stuck

**The first screen (Ben's phone, at move-in):** run 1 had 4 toasts, a level card and Loady at once

## Fun or chore?

| Moment | Fun or chore | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The kit makes the seat's good (sand, copper) from minute one | **Fun** | Identity at once, and the promise in the seat's name is kept. Ben's 36 days of *"sorry ana still no sand"* lose their source |
| *"Ben, the tower wants your sand!"*: a real need from a friend | **Fun** | A goal with faces on it, which run 1's empty Water Tower never was |
| One tap Send, standing, no amount, no deadline, Stop in one tap | **Fun** | One act that keeps working while you sleep: the idle-game version of a gift |
| *"Runs on Ben's sand 💛"* on the receiver's building | **Fun**, if it ever appears | See E4 under (a): it needs a Send |
| Thanks (once a day per pair, counts never shown); the bid (once per need, never "unanswered") | Light | Optional and private. The bid becomes a chore only if needs multiply |
| Sketch | **Fun for Cleo** | A puzzle solved for a friend: the run's warmest conversation, made playable |
| The usual buyer's line *"Copper · 8/min from Cleo, falling 10% a week · Bertha ⚙ sells copper for 2 buildings at 1.4"* (c-friends §1) | **A sting** | It names the friend whose choice cut you, in jargon ("2 buildings at 1.4"). | The receiver's **"Not now"** on a friend's gift (c-friends §1, How a Send ends) | **A sting**, waiting to happen | Declining a friend's gift is a social act. Keep it, but only in "other takers ›", never on the gift's own line |
| Under (b): a friend's sand never arriving while a stranger's does | **Quit-worthy** for the receiver | The game appears to punish friendship. Ana can't fix it with any tap of her own |

## Pass or fail

### c-economy (the land and the money)

| Test | Result | Evidence and fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| E1 | **Pass** | Kits make the seat's good (§3), seat cards tell the truth, and day-1 income is 93–104 g/min for every seat, so Cleo's *"the sunny side feels weaker"* (cleo d01-2000) goes too |
| E3 | Pass | It adds no word. The ladder (1.15 / 1.3 / 1.4 / 2.0) stays invisible, and the only prices a player sees are a friend's and a House's |
| E5 | Pass | Choices come at Level 5–7 (a Glassworks on Ben's sand, a Smelter on Cleo's copper), which is exactly when the run's players were looking for something to build (Ana sat at 9 buildings from day 4 to day 36) |
| **E6** | **Fail** | **With no copies, four of five land goods are missing on every estate,** and the spec doesn't say what the Build menu shows. Today a mine without its deposit says *"needs a … deposit"* on every tile (mia d15-2100 #132–#135). That is how Dev's *"no deposit on my land"* (dev d10-2000 #73, #79) and Ben's *"only found stone here"* felt: a missing feature, then guilt. Under the deal every player meets it on day 2, at Level 5 |

**The fix for E6.** The Build menu lists extractors only for the goods your land holds. The others appear once, greyed,
as one line in the building's own words:

> *"Copper Mine · there's no copper on your land. Cleo (Copper Gulch) makes it, and Bertha ⚙ sells it: build a
> Smelter, and its copper comes by itself."*

The Deposits overlay labels the station's goods by seat ("copper: Cleo"). The no-copies change ships only with this
line, beside the synthesis's guard about the phone gate pipe.

**Verdict:** fails as written, on one line. With it: pass.

### c-friends, answer (a): Send on top of the automatic Yard

| Test | Result | Evidence and fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| E1 | Pass, with one fix | The evening works. But Loady's first-gift step points at a button that may not exist (E4) |
| E2 | **Pass** | Three single taps, no drag inside Send (§6), relief with no tap at all. Run-1 Ben gets *"Your sand kept Ana's Glassworks running for 33 days"*. Two small fixes: the day-2 example cards (§5) show Ana's Glassworks, which needs Level 7, so they should show the Water Tower; and the shared drag-before-gift item (below) |
| E3 | Fail on nouns; pass on verbs and the card | The shared fix: the budget below |
| **E4** | **Fail** | **"Send is shown only when it changes where the goods go"** (§1) is real but unpredictable. Whether the button exists depends on the matcher's order, on House demand and on other takers, none of which a player can see. The common case in a four-seat station (one producer per good, one friend taker, residents served before the project) is "no button". So the heart and *"runs on Ben's sand"*, which need a Send (§3), rarely appear, and the readers (Cleo, Dev), who asked *how do I send*, rarely get to do it |
| E5 | Pass for the skimmer; weak for the readers | E4's fix repairs it. One sting: the usual-buyer line names the friend (above) |

**The fixes for (a):**
1. **Show "Send ▸" whenever a crew mate's building (or the Water Tower) takes your good.** Its real effect is a
   guarantee:
   > *"Send to Ana ▸ · it already goes to her; Send keeps her first if sand runs short, and makes it your gift."*

   Priority is a real change of where goods go, so Cleo can't find a placebo. The button is always there for the same
   reason, so a player can predict it.
2. **Loady's first-gift step completes on the first Send,** and the Send row to the Water Tower always exists while
   the Tower wants the good. That is the existing "project first" choice under its new name.
3. **A Send never cuts a crew mate's running supply.** Between crew mates the protected share doesn't decay; a Send
   only directs new or market-bound spare. The 10% phase-in stays for strangers.
4. **The usual buyer's line drops the name and the jargon:**
   > *"Copper · 8/min now, falling 10% a week · Bertha ⚙ can sell you more at 1.4."*
5. **"Not now" moves off the gift's own line,** into "other takers ›".
6. **Steal from (b):**
   - the handover at move-in, so a newcomer's House flows to crew mates become **Sends** by default (Dev is the missing
     piece, with a heart, with zero extra taps);
   - the Yard panel's two blocks, gifts and totals, without the "Spare / Lacks" rows that confused Cleo (*"why do I pay
     when I have surplus?"*, cleo d21-1900 #301);
   - "first-comer is the host" (§11).

**Verdict:** fails as written (E4). It passes with fixes 1–5; fix 6 is what to steal.

### c-wild, answer (b): friends give, strangers trade

| Test | Result | Evidence and fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| E1 | Pass, with one fix | Loady's Yard-step text (F3) says goods reach neighbours "by itself", which under (b) is false for friends. Rewrite it, or the first lesson is a lie |
| **E2** | **Fail** | The run's own skimmer quits on day 3, before any friend's building wants his good. All five nets (§6) miss him: there is nothing to hand over on evening one, the gift step sits behind the drag, the bid goes to active players only, the Next steps are collapsed on a phone, and the switch acts only after run 2 |
| E3 | Fail | One more noun ("market"), and a rule that runs against intuition. It needs its own explaining line: *"Your crew's goods move when you Send them. Everyone else trades at the market by itself"* (§5). A new player reads that as "my friends' goods don't reach me", which sounds like a bug |
| **E4** | **Pass** | Its real strength: every gift between friends exists because someone chose it |
| **E5** | **Fail for the receiver** | When a friend doesn't tap, a crew mate's building runs on an NPC or a stranger while the friend's spare sits next door, and the receiver has no tap of her own that changes it. The design's honest worst case (§6, §12.1) is the run-1 feeling, now produced on purpose |

**The fix for (b)** is its own switch, decided now: `crewTradesBySendOnly: false`. With the handover and the panel
blocks carried into (a), that *is* (a) with fixes 1–6. A rule whose failure case is the problem we are fixing should
not be the default we test.

**Verdict:** fails.

## My verdict: (a)

**(a), with fixes 1–6 above.** The deciding evidence is the run's own Ben, a phone skimmer who stopped on day 3:
- under (a) his sand runs Ana's Glassworks for a month, and his return card says someone needed him;
- under (b) it runs nobody's.

(b)'s one advantage, that every gift is real, comes into (a) through fix 1: Send is always shown to a crew taker, and
always keeps that friend first.

## The final first-evening budget

**Stay (five nouns, two verbs):**
- **crew**, **seat**, **House ⚙**, **your gate (G)** and **the Water Tower**;
- **Send** and **Thanks**;
- one phrase, not a noun: *"runs on Ben's sand"*.

**Go from the first evening** (they may appear later, when needed):

| Word | Instead |
| --- | --- |
| **station** | *"Capital NW · 4 seats"*; the toolbar says *Neighbours* (F6). Run-1 players read "station" as a shipping tool (*"Sending copper ingots will need a station"*, Cleo, group, day 6) |

**Toasts on the first screen:** one move-in toast that carries the arrival, the kit and the gate pipes, then Loady. The
achievement waits ten minutes. The level card comes after Loady's first step (F10).

**One gesture to remove:** Loady's step 2 on a phone becomes a one-tap planner (*"Lay the pipe to your trading post · 4
tiles · 8 g"*, F2's planner). Then the first-gift step isn't stuck behind the one move Ben couldn't make.

**One line to add, or no-copies doesn't ship:** E6's Build-menu line. In day 2 of the new world, every new player meets
a good their land doesn't have. If the game says *"needs a copper ore deposit"*, they meet a wall. If it says *"Cleo
makes it, and Bertha ⚙ sells it: build a Smelter"*, they meet a friend.
