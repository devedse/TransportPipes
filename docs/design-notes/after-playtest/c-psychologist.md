# Round C · psychologist: the story, played twice

Run 1's 14 rows and 28 sessions, replayed under "Needs from the land, gifts by pipe": once with **(a)**, Send on top
of the automatic Yard (c-friends), and once with **(b)**, friends give only by Send (c-wild). The same people, devices,
budgets and days (`stories/17a.md`, `playtest/players/`).

## What both versions share

- **The land deal for Capital NW** (c-economy §9):
  - Ana: wood, with stone in a Level-5 parcel, on a windy seat. Kit: a Lumber Camp and 3 moilies.
  - Ben: sand. Kit: a Sand Dredger and 3 moilies.
  - Cleo: copper, on a sunny seat. Kit: a Copper Mine and 2 moilies.
  - Dev: coal. His seat is warmed by Coal Co ⚙ until day 8.
- **Mia** on the porch, Capital N: coal, sand and wood, next to Bertha ⚙ (copper and stone). Her kit is a Coal Pit and
  2 moilies.
- **The price** is 1.3, and the project pays 1.3.
- **The fix plan underneath:** F1, F2, F4 (the Foundation is 900 energy, recipes come from what residents make), F5,
  F7, F8, F9 (tending repairs) and F10.
- **Loady's first-gift step.**
- **Pacing:** kits earn about 100 g/min against run 1's 78–90, so unlocks come close to run 1's days:
  - the Smelter (Level 6) on day 3 for Ana and Cleo;
  - the Glassworks (Level 7) on day 4 for Ana;
  - Level 5 on day 10 for Dev.

I separate four feelings:
- **relief:** "I didn't let them down", which needs only the outcome;
- **being needed:** my goods ran a friend's building;
- **giving:** I chose to, for someone;
- **being given to:** someone chose me.

The last two depend on intention, on both sides. Gray (2012) found that the same sweet tasted sweeter when people
believed another person had chosen to give it to them.

**The moment giving is felt is day 8, 20:05.** Dev's first session and his tap on *"Ana's Smelter could use your coal
· Send ▸"*. It has every part:
- a real need he can see;
- a person named;
- a choice;
- an effect at her building (*"Runs on Dev's coal 💛"*);
- his own persona's wish met in minute five.

Both versions produce it. **It needs a consumer on one side and a producer on the other, and that is what the land deal
creates.** Everything before it is relief or "we".

## Where each answer breaks

**(a) Send on top:**
1. **Evening one has no act.** "Shown only when it changes where the goods go" hides every Send before day 4. Loady's
   first-gift step then has no button to show (c-friends §1 against §6).
2. **The button disappears when it would mean the most.** Dev wants to give coal to Cleo, but her Smelter is fully
   supplied, so there's no button and no reason given. Run 1's *"how do I send?"* comes back, a zero with no sentence.
3. **A crew supplier displaces the stranger:** the pro-rata matcher (`Yards.cs` 379–383, 485).

**(b) Friends give, strangers trade:**
1. **A friend who stops before a need exists never gives anything.** Ana pays Bertha 1.4 while Ben's sand sells to the
   hub at 1.0. The crew's keystone is made of a House's sand. Cleo sees it all.
2. **It moves the asking into the chat, where no design rule reaches.** Ana's day-4 message to a friend who has gone
   is exactly the pressure the veto rules exist to prevent. In run 1 it produced guilt in four of Ben's five diaries.
3. **Strangers' trades go unnamed** (*"market totals, no names"*, c-wild §1). So no Thanks reaches Mia, and the lonely
   player is never addressed through what she makes.
4. **A Send to the project is a label.** Cleo finds it on evening one.
5. **The handover net catches nothing here:** no flows on evening one, and Mia undercut Coal Co ⚙ before Dev came.

## Verdict: (a), changed in five places

**(a) wins.** It gives Ben relief and a reason to come back without a tap, Dev usefulness on day one, and Mia a name on
her goods. It produces the same felt gift as (b) at the moment that matters. (b)'s best moment, the return as an act,
can be borrowed.

**What the specs must change:**
1. **Send is always offered where a friend's building or the project uses your good, and honestly labelled.**
   - When it changes no flow today, it reads *"Send ▸ · keeps Ana first"*. It is a standing promise: priority when
     spare gets short, kept by the stand-in under the giver's name.
   - That is real enough for the outsider's test. For the *receiver*, intention is the point (Gray 2012): *"Runs on
     Dev's coal 💛"* is a different social fact from *"via the gate"*, with the same goods.
   - When nobody needs it, the panel says why: *"Cleo's Smelter has all the coal it needs"*.
2. **Evening one's act is a Send to the project,** offered by Loady after the Yard step, as a pledge named in the ring.
   It must work under (a).
3. **Protect usual sellers like usual buyers.** A new crew supplier, by Send or by the matcher, may take at most 10% a
   week of a stranger's 7-day share of a buyer. The design promised "usual sellers first" (§7). The build shares pro
   rata.
4. **Keep strangers' automatic trades named to their two ends,** with Thanks. Reject (b)'s anonymous market for them:
   Mia's warmth came from being addressed.
5. **Take (b)'s return.** A returning giver whose goods ran a friend's building automatically gets one line:
   *"Your sand ran Ana's Glassworks all month · make it yours: Send ▸"*. That is relief first, then an act, with no
   request ever made in his absence. Never show a bid to a friend who isn't lit.

**For run 2's measures:**
- Count **chosen acts** (Sends by a tap, Thanks given, tends, Sketches built) apart from **named flows**.
- Read the diaries for *"I sent"* and *"X needed my…"* separately from *"my sand reached…"*.
- In this replay the line that matters is Dev's on day 8. If run 2 has no session like it by day 10, the land deal is
  working and the act isn't.
