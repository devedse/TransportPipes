# Round B · outsider

The six round-A papers agree on more than they disagree:
- the world layer held;
- the together layer failed by design;
- the fix plan's honesty fixes ship;
- friends need goods that differ, an act of giving, and a better price.

I tested that agreement the way I tested the specs in round 4. For each proposal I asked:
- Would a new player get it on the first evening?
- Does it live where the run showed players look, or where they don't?
- Is it fun or a chore?
- Does it fight another proposal?

**Where players looked in run 1:**
- the building panel (Ana opened her factory's panel in five sessions);
- Loady's card and its *Show me* (Ben did only what *Show me* opened: ben d01-2000 #15, d36-2000 #81–#84);
- the top bar;
- the chat.

**Where they didn't look:** the Station panel (first opened on day 14), the crew chip, the bell and the Plan board (0
clicks in 36 days).

**What they clicked through unread:** the return dialogs (23 "Collect and continue" clicks).

## Answers to the questions put to me

**Wild: is "the Works" plus "Send to" clearer than Yard + project + Plan board + Ask? Can the Plan board go?**
- **The Plan board goes.** 0 pins in 36 days: a pin board, in a game where nobody opens panels.
- **"Send" is clearer than everything it replaces.** It is the players' own word: Dev asked three times *"How do I
  actually send sand to you?"* (group, days 10, 14, 21).
- **"The Works" is no clearer than "project".** I renamed "Works" to "project" in my round-5 review only because the
  example crew was called "Kettle Works". The word was never the problem. The Water Tower failed because it lived in a
  tab nobody opened until day 21 (cleo d21-1900 #302).
- So the best noun is the building's own name: "the Water Tower wants sand", not "the project" or "the Works".

**Friends: is "Send ▸" on the building panel where a three-minute phone player would find it?**
- **Not on its own.** Ben opened a building panel only when *Show me* opened it for him (his solar panel, day 1 and day
  36).
- For Ben, Send must also be:
  - on the first screen he can't skip (the one return card);
  - Loady's step for the first gift, whose *Show me* opens the producer with **Send** lit.
- For Dev and Cleo, who read panels, the building panel is right.
- And for a skimmer, the gift should already flow by default, so that he only has to see the line saying it did.

**Lonely: is the porch clear at a glance, or does it need its own picture?**
- **Drop the word, keep the rule.** "Porch" is one more noun for a thing players never asked about.
- **The picker card says it in plain words:** *"Capital N · next door to Tea Time Pipes (Ana, Ben, Cleo) · you share
  their Yard and their Water Tower."*
- **The picture is the pipe.** Draw the crew's striped Yard pipe running on to Mia's Yard gate, at the edge of her own
  estate, where her camera is.
- An arch is optional art. A stripe in the crew's colours at her gate says more than an arch at a corner she never
  sees.

**Economy: does "you can't make copper here, Cleo can" read as a reason to talk to Cleo, or as a missing feature?**
- **It depends on when it is said and what sits beside it.**
- **As a red error at placement, it is a missing feature.** Compare Dev's *"no deposit on my land, and the overlay
  didn't show one"* (dev d10-2000 #73, #79) and Ben's *"only found stone here"* (group, day 3): both read as walls,
  because nothing said where sand comes from.
- **As an introduction, it is a reason.** It needs three things:
  - the seat card says it on the first evening: *"Copper Gulch: copper and coal. Sand: from Ben, or Bertha ⚙"*;
  - the Build menu says it before you choose: *"Smelter · copper: Cleo sends it, or Bertha ⚙ sells it"*;
  - Cleo is a person who plays. In a station of Houses it must say *"Bertha ⚙ sells copper for one Smelter"*.

**Psychologist: does "Send it / runs on Ben's sand" pass my five-word test better than Ask, Plan board and Links?**
- **Yes:** one verb and one phrase, no noun.
- **But your paper then adds three more phrases**: "I'll bring it", "Let it go" and "I bring it" for pledges. Each is a
  card type a player has to learn. That is the five-word test failing again by other means.
- Keep **Send** and the effect line. A pledge to the Water Tower is a Send to the Water Tower. A promise a friend can't
  keep yet is the seat card's honest path ("sand · a dredger on the shore · Level 5"), not a new card.

## What I attack

**1. Everything we put "in the Yard" will be as invisible as the Water Tower was.**
- Three proposals put their best moment there: wild's Works "goes up in the Yard corner", the psychologist's send is a
  stripe "on the Yard's ring pipe", and friends' line is "drawn across the Yard".
- At the default zoom the Yard corner isn't on screen. It isn't in any estate shot I checked (ana d01-2000-0026,
  d04-2100-0122, d14-1900-0159, d36-2000-0289), and nobody opened the Station panel on the first evening.
- **A gift must show where both people already are:**
  - at the receiver's building: its panel, and its input pellets in the giver's colour;
  - at the giver's gate: their own estate edge;
  - on the return card.
- Or the first evening's camera frames the Yard. But a corner you have to scroll to is a tab with grass on it.

**2. The Works brings a second price system.**
- Wild's feeders get "hub value plus 80% of value added": 1.50 for a Glassworks, 1.40 for a Small Factory, 1.31 for a
  Smelter, 1.27 for a Brickworks.
- Cleo checks every number. She already flagged one unexplained price: *"Iron ore from Ana cost 5.25 g, three times the
  energy price"* (findings, "The Yard panel contradicts itself"; cleo d21-1900 #299).
- Four prices per machine beside "one fair price for everybody" is a puzzle for her and noise for everyone else.
- It also gives to *the station*, not to Ana. The psychologist's point: requests "to the station" spread
  responsibility until nobody owns them.
- And it is the largest build of the five (M–L).
- Its instinct, a running machine fed by friends, is right. But F4's Water Tower, fed by Send, gives the same moment
  with far fewer rules.

**3. Card inflation.**
- In the run, every return began with a stack of dialogs, and players clicked through them: 23 "Collect and continue"
  clicks; Ana: *"Three dialogs in a row blocking everything"*.
- The proposals together would add to the one return card:
  - bids (psychologist);
  - promise cards and "Let it go" (psychologist);
  - pledges (psychologist);
  - "Today here" (lonely);
  - tends that name tenders (lonely);
  - sketches (friends, wild);
  - the Invest line (fix plan).
- F5 has six lines at most. **Give it one social line**: the best one this time (a bid, a sketch, a friend back, a
  stand-in), and the rest folded.

**4. Rules nobody will see, doing work nobody can check.**
- Wild's "crew hands": a helper may lay pipes worth up to 100 g, with a permission flag and a 24-hour undo.
- Lonely's tend that carries a resting friend's spare to the project.
- Economy's Houses that sell land goods at 1.4 "for one level-1 building's need", in a six-step price ladder (1.0, 1.15,
  1.3, 1.4, 2.0, 2.5).
- Friends' friend-line priority: after contracts, before Asks and the Yard.

A rule players can't see is fine only when its result shows in one sentence where they look. *"Bertha ⚙ sells copper
for one Smelter; for more, a copper neighbour"* passes. *"Dev's hands laid a pipe"* passes. A tend that silently moves
goods doesn't. And a permission flag nobody sets is code nobody uses.

**5. A Send that changes nothing is a placebo, and Cleo will notice.**
- If the kits pair up and the honest Yard matches by itself, Ben's sand reaches Ana's Glassworks with or without
  **Send**. Then the button only relabels a flow.
- Cleo checks numbers (*"the numbers are trustworthy"*, cleo d02-2000). She will find out that Send changed nothing.
- **Make Send real, or don't show it:**
  - When the producer isn't piped to the gate, Send lays the pipe. That was Dev's case for eleven days, with his sand
    filling a Storage.
  - When there are two takers, Send chooses one.
  - When the flow already runs, show the effect and a Thanks, not a button.

**6. Daily tending with a job becomes a daily chore.** A tend that matters *every* day turns into "I should tend
Ben", which is the guilt we removed. F9's repair, shown only when there is damage, plus the tenders' names on the
return card, is the right size.

**7. The vocabulary is creeping back.**
- Count the nouns in the six papers: porch, Works, Sketch, bid, pledge, promise card, Today here, friend line, crew
  hands, keystone, Plan board.
- v1.2 failed my word test with about twenty words in the first hour (Ben's first screen piled up seven: ben
  d01-2000-0014). Our merge must hold a budget, or we rebuild the pile.

**8. The fix plan's run-2 marks can pass on kind AI players.**
- "First good knowingly given … named by the giver" can be met by an AI quoting the announcement in its diary. The
  brief warns that AI players "read everything and tend to be kind".
- Count acts instead: Send taps, sketches built and pipes laid to the gate.
- And give one role a skimmer's profile, as Ben had.

## The directions

**A · Gifts with a face** (friends, psychologist, me)
- **Core:**
  - kits keep the seat's promise from minute one;
  - each kit's good has a taker in a friend's estate;
  - one verb, **Send**, which is a pipe and a priority and sits on the building panel and the return card;
  - the receiver's building says *"runs on Ben's sand"*;
  - the Water Tower is fed by Send too.
- **Replaces:** F6's after-the-fact labels, the plan's two §7 triggers ("giving needs an act", "kits get one
  consumer"), Asks, pins and the Plan board.
- **Biggest risk:** a Send that duplicates automatic matching (a placebo), and a ring of needs that breaks when seats
  fill out of order. Obligation comes second: the psychologist's "once a session, never 'unanswered'" bounds it.

**B · The station's machine** (wild)
- **Core:** the Works, a station-owned factory fed by every resident's pipe. It shares its margin with its feeders,
  and gold builds its next machine. It also has Send to as links, hands on a friend's land and crew research.
- **Replaces:** the project's baskets and bonuses, and most of F6.
- **Biggest risk:** a second price system, a collective target that diffuses responsibility, the largest build, and a
  machine that stands off screen in the Yard corner.

**C · Land that needs a neighbour** (economy)
- **Core:** goods are dealt per station with no copies on any estate, the one price is 1.3, Houses sell land goods for
  one building, and the advice follows from the land. The honest Yard does the rest by itself.
- **Replaces:** §14's patches, the price vote and the seat-kit trigger.
- **Biggest risk:** the friends still *do* nothing. The goods flow, but by themselves, which is v1.2 with volume. A
  solo player meets the one-building ceiling in week 2.

**I'd champion A, built on C's land.** C is the best answer to "nothing to give". A is the only answer to "how do I
send sand to you?". B's moment, a building that runs because friends feed it, comes free in A once the receiver's
building says whose goods it runs on. A and C fit together; B competes with both.

## My revised proposal

On top of F0–F11:

1. **Goods that differ, from minute one.**
   - Deal the land per station (C).
   - Each kit holds a producer of the seat's own good, even above the player's level.
   - The seat card says what it makes and where the rest comes from.
   - The first hour (energy, iron) stays universal.
2. **One price that pays both sides on every Era-1 chain.** I move from 1.5 to economy's 1.3, on its table, with
   Houses' land goods above it. The bots confirm with a light profile.
3. **One verb, Send, which is a pipe.**
   - It sits on the producer's panel (*"Send sand to Ana's Glassworks · lay the pipe · 52 g"*), on the return card when
     a friend's building could use your good (once a session), and as Loady's step for the first gift.
   - When the flow already runs, there is no button, only the effect and 💛 Thanks.
4. **The effect where both look.**
   - The receiver's building panel: "runs on Ben's sand".
   - The input pellets in the giver's colour.
   - The giver's return card: "your sand kept Ana's Glassworks running all night".
5. **One shared goal:** F4's Water Tower, fed by Send and named on the "This week" row. No pledges, pins or Plan board.
6. **Mia next door:**
   - In small mode, the stranger is seated in the small station next to the crew. Their Yard and Water Tower are shared,
     and their stripe reaches her gate. The rule is lonely's porch, without the word.
   - Chat gets dates.
   - Run 2 also plays without Jo.
7. **Second priority:** Sketch (crew only, the owner taps) and crew research at 75% off. Dropped: the Works, crew hands,
   promise and pledge cards, daily tend jobs.
8. **A word budget for the first evening:**
   - five nouns: station, crew, your gate, House ⚙, the Water Tower;
   - two verbs: Send, Thanks;
   - everything else waits until it is needed.
9. **Run 2 counts acts** (Send taps, gate pipes laid, sketches built), not diary mentions, and has one skimmer.
