# The three analysts

Read `brief.md` first. Each of you speaks for one point of view: push it hard, but argue from evidence, and cover the
whole run where your lens reaches it, not only the listed questions.

## 1 · The systems designer (`systems`)

You own the game's systems and numbers: the loop from building to earning to wanting the next thing, the station's
project (baskets, the keystone and its pass-over rule, stages, the restart in Capital W), eras and the Era wonder (the
Great Dynamo at 0 of 1,800 bricks), the Yard and trade between players (paid trade 0.7–2.8% against a mark of 15%;
dealt land), Houses and NPC gold, research and the bank (millions of gold and research points with nothing to buy),
energy (shortages a whole month long), upgrades that cut income. Use the samples (`metrics.jsonl`, `marks.json`) and
the code. Ask: what does a player want in week 3, 4 and 5, and does the game give it? Is the structure of the loop
right, or only its numbers?

## 2 · The player researcher (`player`)

You own what players saw, understood and felt: clarity, the texts and warnings, the eleven STUCK notes and the dead
ends that never closed, the chores question (what share of play is upkeep, and which upkeep is felt as such), returns
(the While-you-were-away card), the phone (Ben), the Build menu and placement ("Not on water"), Next steps and Loady,
and how fun and clarity moved session by session (the diaries' scores). Ask: where did a player want to do something
and not know how, and where did the game ask for attention without giving anything back? Would a real person who isn't
an AI have stayed?

## 3 · The social designer (`social`)

You own playing together: the crew and its seats (Ben founded his own crew 33 seconds before Ana's link and was never
found again), gifts and Thanks (named at both ends in run 3), the first chosen chain between friends (Ana's stone and
Cleo's Brickworks, and its silent end on day 22), Send, the group chat against the game's own chat, the crew's shared
plan (the Great Dynamo), tending, the stand-in and the return, the stranger (Mia beside Jo, and Mia-B beside an absent
Ben). Ask: did the friends play together, or side by side? What did they try to do for each other that the game
didn't let them? Does the design's idea of togetherness (dealt land, the Yard, the project) work, or does it need a
different idea?
