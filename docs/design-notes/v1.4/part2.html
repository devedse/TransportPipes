7 · The Yard and your crew

The crew

  • The people who live around a Yard are its crew, one to four of them. Moving in makes you a member; moving away ends it. There are no codes, invites to manage or member limits beyond the four plots.
  • The first resident names the Yard (the crew's name), picks its banner colour and chooses open (anyone may settle on a free plot) or closed (a newcomer asks, and a resident says yes). Open is the default.
  • Every resident is an owner. Once more people live there, each of them can open or close the Yard, accept requests, start a Monument and place decorations. Nobody can be thrown out: an unhappy player moves away.
  • A request waits three days. The player who asked can play elsewhere in the meantime and moves in for free when a resident says yes.
  • A closed Yard stays closed, also when everyone in it stopped playing. Pick for me never sends a newcomer there.
  • The crew has its own chat, its banner on its plots, and the Plan board on the Yard's fence: each resident pins "I make…" and "I need…" with one tap per good.

What makes the Yard special

Trading in the Yard

  • A pipe to your Yard gate connects you. The Yard fills what a neighbour's buildings lack from your spare, and the other way round, every minute, offline too.
  • Inside the Yard you pay your neighbour's crew price, which every seller sets (§8). No fees, and no losses beyond your own pipe.
  • Fair when short: buyers keep the share they had over the last seven days, so nobody's factory stops overnight because a neighbour built more. New demand shares the rest.
  • Your stall shows what you sell, your crew price and a "wanted" sign for what you'd buy.

Building in the Yard

  • Workshops (from Level 10): each resident may put their own buildings on up to 64 tiles of the Yard's ends. A building in the Yard is connected to every resident without pipes, so a Steel Mill there takes one neighbour's ingots and another's coal directly. Workshops belong to whoever built them and move with them.
  • The Depot (from Level 10): the crew's shared storage. You put goods in for the crew; a neighbour takes them at your crew price. It is also where the crew piles goods for the Wonder.
  • Monuments, one site per Era, built with gold by any resident in stages without end (§13), and decorations placed by anyone.
When the last resident leavesThe Yard's own buildings (Monuments, the Depot, decorations) are torn down: they belonged to the crew, and the crew is gone. The last player to leave is warned before they confirm. Workshops always go with their owner.

A Yard of one

A solo player in an empty Yard has all of it: their stall, their 64 tiles of workshops, the Depot and the Monument. Nothing is resized for one (rule 2): the Monument costs a solo player what it costs a crew of four, so four friends who share it pay a quarter each. Pick for me prefers Yards where people play, so a newcomer who wants company finds it.

8 · Trading

Goods change hands in four ways. All four run every minute, offline too, and all of them pay in gold for goods that were really made:

The market

Your trading post sells everything to the town's market and can buy from it. It pays the base price up to your Hub Order and less beyond it; it sells at 2.5×.

Your Yard

What a neighbour's buildings lack is filled from your spare, and the other way round, at the price each seller sets for the crew. No contracts, no clicks.

Links

Standing deals with named players in other Yards of your town: a good, an amount an hour, a price.

The Exchange

List goods from your storages for every town, or buy what others list. Goods travel by cargo, and 4% leaves the game.

Gifts to your Yard's Monument and your town's Wonder are a fifth way goods leave your plot. They pay no gold (§13).

The market

Each town has a market in its hub. It buys every good of the town's Era and older, and sells the same goods.

  • Your Hub Order is how much of a good the market buys from you at the full base price each minute. It is yours alone: nobody else's sales change your price, and nobody shares it.
  • Every Era-1 good has a Hub Order worth 1,000 gold a minute at its base price, and every good of a later Era four times as much as one Era before. When your town reaches a later Era, the older goods' Hub Orders grow by half for each Era since.
  • Your trading post's level raises every Hub Order by 10% a level, to 9.85 times at level 25. Upgrading the post is how you grow your market. The post has no other limit: it sells everything that reaches it.
  • Beyond your Hub Order the price falls. Selling twice the Hub Order pays 1.41 times as much, four times pays twice as much, sixteen times four times as much. From sixteen times on, every further unit pays a quarter of the base price.
  • The market sells every good of the town's Era and older at 2.5 times the base price, without limit, to any building whose owner allows imports of that good.
  • These numbers are the same in every town, on every server, whatever the number of players (rule 2).

Worked example: Ben's iron ingots

Ben's trading post is at level 10, so his Hub Orders are 2.36 times the base: 168 iron ingots a minute (1,000 g ÷ 14 g × 2.36). His factories make 500 a minute, three times that. The market pays him 14 × √(168 × 500) = 4,060 gold a minute, an average of 8.1 g an ingot instead of 14. Each extra ingot now adds only 4.1 g. So Ben sells more of his ingots to his crew (who use them for levels, §9), upgrades his trading post, or puts the spare in the Wonder. The game shows each of these on the ingots' row.

Your prices

  • For every good you sell to players you set a price between the market's two prices, 1× to 2.5× the base, so trading with a friend is never worse than the market for either side.
  • A public price for everyone (default 1.3×), a crew price for your Yard (default 1.15×), and special prices for named players ("Ben: 1.0×"). A buyer always pays the lowest of the prices that apply to them.
  • Price rises wait an hour, and the buyers are told. Cuts count at once.
  • There are no free gifts between players, and gold never moves except as the price of goods that were really made. The lowest price (1.0×) is what the market would pay you anyway. That, the price band and the one-hour rule are the guards against a second account feeding the first.

The Yard, minute by minute

Trading in the Yard needs one thing: a pipe from your buildings to your Yard gate. From then on, the game does this every minute, for every good:

  1. Your own first. Your own buildings use what your own buildings make. What they can't use is your spare; what they need and don't get is your want. A want also counts what your storages keep for your next level or building.
  2. Links next. Standing deals with other Yards are filled before the Yard.
  3. Then the Yard. Each resident's want is filled from the others' spare, the seller with the lowest price for that buyer first. With equal prices, the seller who supplied that buyer before goes first, so supplies are steady.
  4. Fair when short. If the spare doesn't cover all wants, each buyer first gets the share it had over the last seven days, and new demand shares what's left. Nobody's factory stops overnight because a neighbour built more.
  5. Through the pipes. The goods run from the seller's buildings to the seller's gate, across the Yard, and from the buyer's gate to the buyer's buildings. Each side loses its own pipe's loss. The buyer pays for what arrives at their gate, at the seller's price, each minute.
  6. What's still wanted comes from the buyer's storages, then from the market at 2.5× if the buyer allows imports of that good; otherwise the building slows down and says why.
  7. What's still spare goes to the seller's storages' keeps, to the Wonder or Monument if "give" is on for that good, then to the market through the trading post, then to storage. When everything is full, the building slows down. Nothing is ever destroyed.

Links: trading across the town

  • From Level 12, a link joins you to one named player in another Yard of your town: a pipe along the roads, laid automatically, paid per tile by its tier, losing a little per tile as every pipe does.
  • A link is a standing deal: a good, an amount per hour and a price (the seller's special or public price). It comes before the Yard in the order above, so it is reliable.
  • Up to 12 links. A link has a two-hour tank at the buyer's end, so a short gap never stops a factory.
  • From Era 3, trunk lines carry a Yard's links to other towns (§11).

The Exchange: trading with every town

  • From Level 12 you can list goods from your storages on the Exchange, at a price within the band, or buy what others list, in any town. It is how towns trade with each other from the start.
  • Goods travel by cargo: about an hour to the next town, longer further away (a Cargo Depot halves it, trains in Era 3 again). A 4% fee is taken out of the game.
  • You can only buy goods of your own Era or older.

Where your goods go

A building's spare, in this order

  1. Your own buildings on the same pipes
  2. Your links
  3. The Yard: neighbours who want it
  4. What your storages keep (today's orders, your next level or building, a pile you set)
  5. The Wonder or a Monument, for goods you switched to "give"
  6. Your trading post: the market
  7. Your storages, until full
  8. Nowhere left: the building slows down. Nothing is wasted.

A building's need, in this order

  1. Your own buildings on the same pipes
  2. Your links
  3. The Yard: the cheapest neighbour first
  4. Your storages
  5. The market at 2.5×, if you allow imports

9 · Buildings, levels and pipes

  • Placement: on your bought land, on fitting terrain (a mine needs its deposit, a pump a shore). Footprints are 1×1, 2×2 or 3×3. A building costs gold, and from Era 2 also goods; it takes seconds in Era 1 and hours in Era 5, with scaffolding and a crane.
  • Only pipes connect. Buildings that touch a pipe trade over it. Goods never pass through a building.
  • Moving your own buildings is free (a short rebuild). Demolishing returns half the gold, all of it with a Recycler.
  • Copies: every copy of the same building costs 7% more than the last (the 20th Wind Moily 723 g, the 40th 2,800 g), so you grow both by building more and by levelling up, and a plot of many kinds beats a plot of one.
  • Evolve in place: a building with a successor (Wind Moily → Big Turbine, Coal Plant → Steam Turbine, Solar Panel → Solar Farm, Small Factory → Big Factory, Storage → Warehouse → Silo, Trading Post → Market Hall) grows where it stands. The old building's gold and levels count towards the new one, and the pipes stay.

Levels stop at 25

  • Every level adds 10% output (and input), so level 25 makes 9.85× level 1.
  • A level step costs what that copy cost to build, and 15% more for every level it already has: the step from 24 to 25 costs 25 times the copy's price, and all 24 steps together 184 times. So your 40th Wind Moily's levels cost 14 times your first one's, and growing means building wider, not only higher.
  • From level 11, levels also need goods worth 30% of the step's gold, made in the building's own Era: planks and bricks for the steps to levels 11–15, then also iron ingots and glass (16–20), then also copper ingots (21–25). An Era-2 building needs steel and concrete, then copper wire, then aluminium. Your storages keep what your next level needs; what you don't make comes from your Yard at your neighbours' price or from the market at 2.5×. Every plot lacks one basic, so every plot needs a friend's bricks, glass or copper ingots to grow.
  • A building shows a star at levels 5, 10, 15, 20 and 25, and looks grander at each (bronze, silver, gold, copper trim, and at 25 the owner's pennant with a golden glow).
  • The cap is the point: with a fixed plot and a fixed top level, a plot has a ceiling we can calculate. Research then matters as much as levels, and every big price in the game is set against that ceiling.

Sleeping buildings

A building from a later Era than your town's sleeps: it shows a moon, makes and uses nothing, and costs nothing. It wakes when the town reaches its Era. This happens when you move to a town one Era below your own (§11).

One pipe, every kind of transport

The pipe is the game's heart, so it should show what it does. In 1.4 the pipe is a big conduit with three lanes inside: power cables, a conveyor belt and a slim fluid line. Where it plugs into a building, the lane that building needs comes out through a brass hatch on the side of the pipe, so you can read a plot's connections at a glance.

The rules

  • One connector per kind. A building gets a connector for each kind of good it takes or makes: a Wind Moily cables; an Iron Mine cables and a belt; a Water Pump cables and a slim pipe; a Coal Plant all three. A Storage or trading post shows the kinds that are moving through it right now.
  • They move only when goods move. The belt rolls in the direction of the flow, the sparks run towards whoever uses the energy, the fluid glints. A building that stands still has still connectors, so an idle building shows at once.
  • The big pipe keeps its glowing pellets in the goods' colours. The connectors show what kind of transport it is; the pellets show which good.
  • The pipe's tier shows on the hatches and connectors too: iron, copper, blue steel, glass, violet superconductor trim.
  • Look inside: hovering a pipe (or holding Alt) turns it see-through, like the cutaway, showing what runs in each lane and how full it is.
  • Zoomed out, the connectors fade so the map stays calm; close up they're one of the nicest things to watch.
  • Power lines (Era 2) plug into the pipe's cable lane where they meet it.

How it's made: each good gets a kind in the content files (power, solid or fluid). The connectors are rendered pieces, like the pipes themselves (art/pipes.mjs), not generated pictures, so they line up exactly with every pipe shape and building plate: three kinds, four directions, one trim per tier, side by side when a building needs more than one. The goods on the belts are the resource icons. These Codex mock-ups set the look.

10 · Resources and chains

Twenty-nine goods, five Eras. Every Era brings one new land resource and three or four goods made from it and from older goods. One rule sets all the numbers: a building adds about four times as much value per tile as one of the Era before (25 g per tile per minute in Era 1, 100 in Era 2, 400, 1,600 and 6,400 in Era 5).
