TransportPipes · the complete game design · version 1.4

TransportPipes 1.4

You start with an empty plot next to a Yard. You build mines, factories and pipes, sell to the market and trade with your Yard's neighbours at prices you set. Your research makes you very good at a few things, so friends who split the work grow faster. Together the town builds a Wonder, and the Wonder takes the whole town into the next Era, with new resources, new buildings and more room.

Your own placePick a free plot on the map, next to friends or anywhere. Start with an HQ, a trading post and 2,000 gold.
The YardFour plots share one Yard of 32×16 tiles. The people who live there are a crew, and they own the Yard together.
Only peopleThe market is the only player that isn't a person. It always buys and sells, but friends beat its prices.
Deep researchA research track takes about ten days and makes its chain fifteen times better. Basic tiers take minutes, the big ones days.
Towns and ErasSixteen players make a town. Each town reaches its next Era by finishing its own Wonder.
A ceiling you can seeBuildings stop at level 25. Every Era makes about four times the one before.

One balance at every size

No number in the game depends on how many people play (rule 2): no quiet-server bonus, no Wonder or Monument sized to the active players, no "half the town" rule. A bigger town gets further because more people make more: Era 2 begins on day {{E2:16}} in a town of sixteen regular players, day {{E2:8}} with eight, day {{E2:4}} with four and day {{E2:1}} for someone alone.

Gold always has somewhere to go

An engaged player builds out all the land they own within about four days, whatever the prices, because every visit spends hours of saved income. So each Era ends in a late phase: the Yard's Monument takes gold in stages without end (each makes every resident 3% more productive), and the town's Wonder takes goods, or gold at the market's 2.5×. From day 5 to day 34 a regular player spends {{SPENT}} of what they earn, and at midnight holds {{BANK}} of income.

Trading

The market buys everything and sells everything at 2.5×; friends trade between the two prices, so both sides win. Because every plot can do Era 1 alone (rule 1), paid trade in a crew is {{TRADE:lo}} to {{TRADE:hi}} of its income. A crew is {{CREW:7}} ahead of the same four players alone by day 7 and {{CREW:14}} by day 14: {{CREW:trade}} of that lead is trade, the rest the Monument they share. Goods for levels from level 11 give every plot a reason to buy from a friend.

Research

Going deep beats spreading for every track: a master earns {{TRACKS:21}} more than the same plot researching everything a tier at a time by day 21, and the tracks end within {{SPREAD:35}} times of each other. That needed three rules: bigger Hub Orders for masters from tier 5, coal in the Copper and Stone tracks, and the Power track at half (§12).

What the last round settled

What pipes carry (100, 5,000 and 50,000 a minute between two buildings), how a level's goods come in, the Era tree's costs, Deep Mines on copper and coal, the Big Factory, the wilderness and other towns (§20), and the rest of the open items. All of it is listed in §25.

Left open on purpose: the numbers of Eras 3 to 5, which phase C sets when the model plays them. The bots in phase B play the real server and must land within a fifth of the model's numbers (§23).

1 · The game in one minute

  1. You pick a plot. The start screen is the map. You click a free plot of 32×32 tiles in a town that is still in Era 1, next to a friend or wherever you like.
  2. You build from nothing. Wind Moilies and solar panels make energy, mines dig ore, factories turn it into ingots, bricks and glass. Pipes connect them, and goods flow every minute, also while you're away.
  3. You sell. Your trading post sells to the town's market. The market pays the full price for an amount of each good (your Hub Order) and less for floods. It also sells everything, at 2.5 times the price.
  4. You trade with your Yard. Your plot touches a Yard shared with three neighbours. A pipe to your Yard gate is all it takes: what a neighbour lacks is filled from your spare at the price you set, and the other way round. Every price between friends beats the market for both sides.
  5. Nobody has everything. Each plot lacks two of the eight land resources, a different two for each plot in a Yard. Any three neighbours together have everything.
  6. You get very good at a few things. Research tracks take days. A player who masters the Iron track makes fifteen times the iron of someone who didn't, with the same buildings, and the market buys more from a master. Friends who master different tracks sell each other what each lacks, and the four of them pay for one Monument that makes all four more productive.
  7. Your town moves on together. Sixteen players share a town and its market. The town builds its Era Wonder from the goods its players give. When it's finished, the whole town enters the next Era: a new raw resource, new goods, new buildings, a better pipe and more room. After five Eras the town launches its Space Elevator.

2 · Seven rules the game follows

Every part of this design was checked against these. When two ideas fight, the rule higher in the list wins.

RuleWhat it meansWhere you see it
1 · Fun alone, better with friends A solo player can reach every Era and finish every track. Friends who split the work get there sooner and earn more. Nobody is ever stuck because a friend stopped playing. The market always buys and sells; research by time; the Yard's Monument shared by its residents
2 · One balance at every size No number in the game depends on how many people play: not on the server, not in the town, not in the Yard. The game never makes up for having fewer people. More people get further because more people make more, never because a rule changes. Fixed Hub Orders, fixed Wonder and Monument sizes, no "quiet server" bonus, no "half the town" rule
3 · Only people No computer-run companies. The market is the one thing that isn't a person, and it never pretends to be one. Free plots are empty land; no Houses, stand-ins or caretakers
4 · Your own place You choose where you live, start from nothing, and nobody can touch what you built. Pick a spot on the map; moving is free; no destructive actions
5 · Everything flows Goods visibly move through pipes. You can tell what a plot does by looking at it. Pipes, the Yard's stalls, goods crossing the Yard
6 · Choices that matter What you research, which land you buy, who in the Yard makes what and how much you give the Wonder all change how your game goes. Research takes days; level cap; land lacks two resources
7 · Progress while away Everything keeps running offline. Coming back is a reward. Nothing is lost by not playing. Offline report, research and Wonders that wait for you

3 · Words

Plot
Your land: 32×32 tiles, in 16 parcels of 8×8. You start with four parcels (16×16) against the Yard.
Parcel
8×8 tiles of land. You buy them one at a time, each 2.5 times the price of the one before, one more every second Company Level.
Yard
The 32×16 space between four plots. Its residents trade there, build Monuments there and own it together.
Crew
The people who live around one Yard (one to four). Joining a Yard means joining its crew.
Yard gate
Where your plot meets the Yard. A pipe from your buildings to your gate is your connection to the Yard.
Town
Four Yards (16 plots) around a hub. A town has one market, one Era and one Era Wonder. The first town is the Capital, and every town has a name (Ironvale, Copperbrook…).
Hub
The town's centre: the market, the Wonder site, statues and the town board.
Market
The town's buyer and seller of everything. It pays a good's base price, up to your Hub Order, and sells at 2.5× the base.
Hub Order
How much of a good the market buys from you per minute at the full price. Beyond it, the price falls.
Outskirts
The land between a town's Yards. From Era 2, each plot can buy nine outskirts parcels set aside for it.
Era
A stage of a town: Steam & Iron, Electric, Oil & Machines, Electronics, Space Age. Your own Era is the highest Era you have reached.
Era Wonder
The town's big building project. Finishing it moves the town into the next Era.
Monument
A Yard's own building project, one site per Era, built with gold in stages without end; every stage gives every resident a perk.
Track
A research line for one chain (Power, Iron, Sand…), nine tiers over about ten days.
Base price
A good's reference price. Every price in the game is a multiple of it.

4 · The world: plot, Yard, town

A Yard group

  • The Yard is 32×16 tiles, wider than high, five times the size of today's Yard.
  • One plot sits above it and one below, sharing the Yard's long sides. Two more sit at its ends.
  • Every plot's start area (its four first parcels, 16×16) lies against the Yard, with the Yard gate in the middle of that edge. Your first pipe reaches the Yard.
  • The plots above and below get 32 tiles of Yard edge, the ones at the ends 16. That changes only how much Yard edge each has, not what they can trade.

A town

  • Four Yard groups, so 16 plots, around the hub (68×52 tiles): the market, the Era Wonder's building site, statues and past Wonders.
  • Two roads cross the town. Links between Yards run along them.
  • The gaps between the groups are the outskirts: twelve patches of 32×24 tiles, 144 parcels. Each plot has nine of them set aside for it (§5).
  • Around each town lies wilderness, 128 tiles to the next town, with rivers, mountains and rich deposits. Where the roads leave the town stand four town gates, where lines from other towns arrive (§20).

The world

  • Towns spiral out from the Capital, the first town. The Capital is a town like the others; it's just the oldest.
  • A new town opens when every Era-1 town has fewer than two free plots. Newcomers always start in an Era-1 town, so there is always one with room.
  • The world map shows every town with its name, its Era, its Wonder's progress and how many plots are free.
  • Towns lie 128 tiles apart, four times today's distance, so there's room between them for trunk lines (Era 3) and outposts (Era 4). Until then towns trade over the Exchange (§20).

5 · Your land

What every plot has, and what it lacks

There are eight land resources besides the ones every plot has. Each plot has six of them, and lacks one Era-1 basic and one later resource:

Every plot hasEach plot lacks one of…and one of
water, iron ore, wood (forest), wind, sun copper ore · coal · stone · sand bauxite (Era 2) · oil (Era 3) · rare earth (Era 4) · titanium ore (Era 5)
  • In a Yard the four plots lack different ones, so any three neighbours together have everything. Who lacks what is dealt per Yard when the world is made, and the plot's card shows it before you pick it.
  • The first hour is the same for everyone: every plot has wind, sun, water and iron ore.
  • A missing resource is a nudge, not a wall. You buy it from a neighbour at their price, from the market at 2.5×, from your outskirts (they hold every resource) or, much later, you seed it with a Terraformer.
  • The resources of later Eras are on the land from the start, as sleeping deposits with a small Era badge. You can see what you'll mine in Era 3 when you pick the plot.
  • Each plot has one rich deposit (★4) of something it has: its strength, also on its card. Other deposits are ★1 to ★3. Richness multiplies what a mine digs.

Parcels

  • 16 parcels of 8×8. You start with the four against the Yard, with your HQ and trading post next to the gate.
  • You buy the other twelve in any order, as long as the new parcel touches land you own. Choosing which one is a real decision: the beach for sand, or the hill for wind?
  • The first costs 20,000 gold and each one 2.5 times the last (the twelfth: 477 million). Company Level limits how many you may own: the 5th parcel at Level 5, then one more every second level, the 16th at Level 27. A regular player has the whole plot around day 18 (§17).
  • Pipes may cross parcels you haven't bought, so the Yard gate is always in reach. Buildings need bought land.

The outskirts (from Era 2)

  • Each plot has nine outskirts parcels set aside for it, next to its own land, as the picture shows. Only you can buy yours, so a player in the middle of a town has the same room as one at the edge.
  • They open when your town reaches Era 2 and you've researched Survey the outskirts. The first costs 100 million gold, each one 1.4 times the last (the ninth: 1.5 billion): the big land purchase of Era 2. A regular player has all nine about a week into Era 2 (§17).
  • The outskirts hold every land resource at ★2–★3, so a solo player can make up for what their plot lacks.
  • They count as your plot: same pipes, same buildings. When you move, they're bought back for half their price (§18).

Later Eras add more room in other directions: the Deep Level under your plot in Era 3, and outposts in the wilderness in Era 4.
