15 · Alone and together

Playing alone

  • The market buys everything you make and sells everything you lack. Your plot has six of the eight land resources, plus water, iron ore and wood, so it can do all of Era 1 by itself.
  • A missing resource costs you, but doesn't block you. A Smelter on coal bought from the market at 2.5× still adds 10 g per tile instead of 25. From Era 2 your outskirts hold every resource.
  • You research like everyone: the first tiers of every track, then one track to the top (§12).
  • Nothing is made smaller because you're alone (rule 2). Your Monument costs what it costs a crew, and your town's Wonder what it costs a full town. A lone player in an empty town reaches Era 2 around day {{E2:1}}; in a town with others much sooner, and anyone can move to a livelier town of their Era.
  • You can trade with anyone: links to other Yards, the Exchange to every town.

Playing together: what a crew gets

Ana, Ben, Cleo and Dev share a Yard. Each lacks a different basic; on day one they pin their plans on the Plan board: Ana goes deep in Power, Ben in Iron, Cleo in Sand and Dev in Copper. 16 · Gold and goods: where they go

The last AI playtest showed players spending 0.2% of their gold: it piled up with nowhere to go. In this design gold always has a next step, and the model in §17 checks it: from day 5 to day 34 a regular player spends {{SPENT}} of everything they earn, and at midnight holds {{BANK}} of income.

Gold comes from

  • The market, up to your Hub Orders and less beyond them
  • Other players paying your prices
  • Daily orders, Wonder rewards, expeditions, achievements

Goods go to

  • Your own and your friends' buildings
  • Your and your friends' levels from level 11 (§9)
  • The market
  • The Wonder and missions (gone for good)
  • Research tiers and the costs of Era 2–5 buildings (gone for good)

The ceiling, and what comes after it

A building tops out at 9.85× from levels and 15× from a mastered track; a plot at 16 parcels and, from Era 2, nine outskirts. So a plot has a ceiling in each Era, and an engaged player reaches most of it in about five days. After that, every level and copy left takes more than a week to pay for itself. The Era's late phase answers it: the Monument turns gold into a little more for every resident, the Wonder turns it into the next Era, and the next Era brings new buildings, goods, land and tracks, so paybacks fall back to hours. Big prices are set from the model's incomes, never from how many people play.

17 · A base, day by day: the numbers worked out

Every number in this document was checked by playing it. A model plays a plot minute by minute with exactly the rules of §8 to §16 and the formulas of §22: the land and its deposits, wind and sun, Hub Orders, copies and levels and their goods, what pipes carry, research on both lines (the tracks and the Era tree), Company Level, parcels, the outskirts, the Monument and the Wonder. This section shows what came out: what a base looks like, what it earns, and where its gold goes. The model's code is in docs/design-notes/v1.4/sim, with how to run it.

The player in the model
  • Visits three times a day: ten minutes at 8:00, forty-five at 19:30, ten at 22:30. The first evening is two hours.
  • Does up to six things a minute (two on the first evening, while it learns): place a building, Max one building, start research or buy a parcel.
  • Always buys what pays for itself soonest, keeps research running, buys land when it's useful, and ignores anything that takes longer than five days to pay back.
  • When nothing pays back within five days, puts its gold into the Yard's Monument and the town's Wonder, half each, and keeps two hours of income.
  • Lacks sand (and oil), has its rich deposit in iron, and researches the first two tiers of every track, then Iron to the top, then Steel once Era 2 opens. Its second line does the Era tree, copper pipes first.

Not in the model: daily orders, Wonder rewards, expeditions and events. Together they are about a day of extra income per Era for a regular player in a town of eight, which a real player spends in the late phase.

It is a tireless and sensible player, not a person: it never forgets, never plays for fun, never decorates. People will be slower and less exact. Its numbers are the ceiling of normal play, which is what the prices have to be set against. The bots in phase B play the same rules on the real server and must agree (§23).

Three ways of playing

{{EC:styles}}

A once-a-day player who uses the game's one-tap helpers (Max on a building, Grow on the plot: spend on whatever pays back soonest) keeps up with a regular one, apart from a few days at the start of each Era, because what holds a plot back after the first days is the next parcel, the next research tier and the next Era, not the clicks. With half the clicks (two things a minute), the same player is a day or two behind and ends a visit with up to two and a half days of income unspent. The helpers are what let a short visit spend it all, and they are part of phase A.

The rhythm of an Era

  1. Building up (days 0–2). Everything pays back in minutes, then hours. Gold goes to buildings, levels, research and parcels. Income grows almost twentyfold from day 1 to day 3.
  2. Filling the plot (days 2–4). The start area and the first parcels are built and mostly at level 15 to 25. The next level of anything takes days to pay back; the next parcel waits for the next Company Level.
  3. The Era's late phase (day 4 to the Wonder). Gold that would only buy week-long paybacks goes to the Yard's Monument (every stage makes every resident 3% more productive) and the town's Wonder (the next Era). New parcels and research tiers keep coming and are bought at once. Gold never piles up: from day 5 to day 34 the player spends {{SPENT}} of what they earn.
  4. The next Era (day 12 in a town of eight). New buildings, new goods with fresh Hub Orders, the outskirts and new tracks: paybacks drop to hours again, and income nearly triples within four days. Then the same rhythm.
Why gold no longer piles up

In the AI playtests players spent 0.2% of their gold. The model shows why any version of this game would end that way: a plot is a fixed size, levels stop at 25 and the market pays less for floods, so after a few days nothing a player can buy pays back within a week, however the prices are set. Making things dearer only moves the moment. The answer is the late phase: a Monument that never runs out of stages and a Wonder that leads somewhere, both sized in gold the plot really makes, and the next Era as the reward.

18 · Away, moving and leaving

Away

  • Everything keeps running: buildings, trade, research and gifts with "give" on. Coming back shows the offline report: what you earned, what filled up, what finished.
  • Nothing is lost by not playing, and nothing changes for your neighbours: your plot keeps making and buying what it did. A link's two-hour tank and the Yard's fallback cover any gap.
  • Your plot stays yours, however long you're gone. Towns open new land for newcomers instead.

Moving

  • Free, five minutes between moves, to any free plot in a town of your Era or one below (into a closed Yard with a resident's yes).
  • Your plot is packed and unpacked in the new place: the layout stays where the land allows, and buildings that don't fit (a mine with no deposit there) become crates with their levels, placed later or scrapped for 75%. Workshops come along as crates.
  • Your outskirts parcels are bought back for half their price, their buildings into crates. Your gold, research, levels, Era and titles come with you.
  • The plot you left is empty land again.

Leaving a Yard

  • Moving away ends your crew membership. The last resident to leave is warned that the Yard's Monuments, Depot and decorations will be torn down, and confirms.
  • A Yard whose residents all stopped playing stays as it is, closed or open. Pick for me never sends anyone to a Yard where nobody plays.

19 · Everything else in the game

Loady and the Next steps card

Loady guides the first hour and opens a chapter at each Era: what's new and where to start. The Next steps card always shows the three best things to do, worked out from your real networks.

Daily orders

Three orders a day from the market, for goods you make (from Level 3). Together they ask for about half an hour of your income, each at most what your trading post sells of that good in eight hours, so they fill by themselves; they pay twice the base price for what they take. As in today's game.

Events

Storms (wind ×2; a tenth of the wind buildings run at half speed until repaired, which a storm's extra wind more than pays for), heatwaves (sun ×1.5), droughts, cold snaps (the market wants energy), expos (a good sells for three times as much for six hours), shortages, and from Era 4 Discoveries in the wilderness. Announced a day ahead, three with a Weather Station. As in today's game.

Managers

Jo repairs storm damage, Bea sells what overflows, Rick upgrades what pays back within two hours, Lin renews good links, Sam keeps your research queues full. Slots at Levels 10, 20, 30 and 45, plus one from Automation (Era 4). Max and Grow do what Rick does in one tap, for everyone (§17).

The Deep Level (Era 3)

A second 32×32 layer under your plot. Parcels are dug out, hours for the first and days for the last, giving stone and ore while they dig. Rich ★3–★5 ore, magma for energy, a Vault, pipes that lose nothing. Mines, power and storage only: factories stay above ground. Deep Lifts carry goods up.

The wilderness (Era 4)

Up to three outposts of 8×8 on rich deposits between the towns, bought once. Discoveries add new deposits every four days. Goods come home by Cargo Depot or Quantum Link (§20).

Expeditions (Level 20)

Send a team out for 1, 4 or 12 hours: gold, decorations, relics with a story, blueprints; from Era 4 also where the next rich deposit lies.

Showing off

Decorations, golden collection pieces, buildings that grow grander at every five levels, photo mode, visiting and likes, and the rankings of Yards and towns.

Talking

Yard chat, town chat, world chat and direct messages, with blocking and reporting.

Blueprints and achievements

Save a layout and rebuild it in one go. Achievements and collections give gold, titles and golden decorations.

20 · The wilderness and other towns

Towns lie 128 tiles apart. Until Era 3 they deal with each other over the Exchange. From Era 3 players build trunk lines through the wilderness, and from Era 4 the wilderness itself is worth claiming. All of this belongs to Eras 3 and 4 and is built in phase C; phases A and B only lay out the land, the rivers and mountains, and the town gates, so nothing has to move later.

Eras 1 and 2: the Exchange

  • The Exchange (Level 12) trades with every town by cargo that takes time, with a 4% fee (§8).
  • Fog: the wilderness starts unexplored on your map. Expeditions (Level 20) map it piece by piece and find good routes, deposits and ruins with relics, so by Era 3 you know where a line could run.
  • The world map shows every town's Era and Wonder: the race between towns.

Era 4: the wilderness

  • Outposts: up to three claims of 8×8 on rich deposits, bought once and yours for good. Goods come home by Cargo Depot or Quantum Link.
  • Discoveries every four days add new rich deposits somewhere in the wilderness, so there is always land left to claim.

Trunk lines (Era 3)

  • Anyone can build one who has researched Trunk Lines. A line runs from a town gate of your town through the wilderness to a town gate of another town, and it belongs to you: you are its carrier.
  • Right of way: you buy the tiles your line runs on, once. There is no rent, so nothing runs out while you're away (rule 7). Lines may cross each other with a bridge but never share a tile, and whoever builds a route first has it.
  • What it carries: links (§8) between players of the two towns, and sales to the other town's market. There your Hub Orders are half of what your own town's market gives you, so a line is a second market. Goods only go to towns of their Era or later.
  • Fees: the carrier sets them like prices: a public fee, a crew fee and special fees for named players, from nothing to 10% of the goods' base value, paid by the seller. When a line is full, its usual users keep the share they had over the last seven days, as in the Yard.
  • Loss: a line loses what any pipe of its tier loses: over 128 tiles a composite line delivers 97%, a glass conduit 99%, a superconductor line everything.
  • A line stays where it is, keeps running and stays its carrier's, also when they move to another town or stop playing.
  • The Lines track (Era 3, the 21st track) is for carriers: every tier makes your lines and links cheaper a tile and lose less, and its master tier lets the Pipe Master build superconductor lines (no loss, no limit) from Era 3. A Yard's Pipe Master is its door to other towns, as its power specialist is its energy.
  • Why people pay a carrier: players in another town to trade with, a second market, goods their own town hardly makes, and old towns' hunger for young towns' basics: an Era-4 town's chains eat ingots and bricks, so a new town has buyers from its first week.

24 · What could go wrong

Friends trade less than you'd hopeEvery plot can do Era 1 by itself (rule 1), so in the model trade between friends is {{TRADE:lo}} to {{TRADE:hi}} of a crew's income, about what the playtests saw. 1.4 doesn't pretend otherwise. Still, trade is {{CREW:trade}} of a crew's lead (the shared Monument the rest), and the goods for levels give friends something real to sell each other from the second week. If people want more trade, the levers are the share of goods in levels (30% now) and how many resources a plot lacks.
  • The Era's late phase feels flat. After about four days the land you own is built and its income grows slowly until the Wonder. Research tiers, Monument stages, the Wonder's stages and new parcels keep something happening at every visit, and §17 shows gold never piles up; whether it's fun is for people to tell us.
  • The model is a bot. It plays tirelessly and well. People will be slower, so the real pace is a little behind §17, never ahead. The helpers (Max, Grow) close most of the gap, and phase B's bots check the real server against the model.
  • Tracks aren't equal on the real server. In the model they end within {{SPREAD:35}} times of each other (§12). The bots in phase B play every track on the real server; if one falls behind, the levers are its Hub Order growth and the Power track's share.
  • Ten-day tracks feel slow. Tiers 1–4 finish on day one, the Era tree runs beside them, and the research panel shows what each tier will add in gold a minute, so a two-day tier is something to look forward to.
  • A lone player in a quiet town waits long for Era 2. About day {{E2:1}} by themselves, against day 12 in a town of eight. That's rule 2 at work. Pick for me never sends newcomers to an empty town, and anyone can move to a livelier town of their Era for free.
  • A late friend lands in a different town. They can trade over the Exchange from day one, the trail makes their Wonders smaller, and the older friend can move down one Era to join them.
  • Numbers get huge. A plot earns millions a minute in Era 2 and Wonders cost billions. The interface already shortens numbers (12.4M), and idle players like seeing them grow.
  • A lot of today's code goes. Houses, stand-ins, seats, kits, corporations, contracts, quiet grace and more. The solver, the pipes and the art stay, and every phase is playable on its own.
  • Bigger worlds per player. Plots stay 32×32, but with the outskirts and the Deep Level a late plot is about 2.5 times as many tiles, and the model's plot has 800 buildings on day 34. The big-estate check measures it in phases A and C.

25 · Decisions

Everything in this document is decided, and phase A can start from it as written. These are the choices I made for you, newest first, so they're the ones worth a look. Any of them can still change.

In the last round

  1. Pipes carry 100 (grey), 5,000 (copper) and 50,000 (steel) a minute between two buildings. The grey pipe fills on the first evening and teaches copper pipes; after that only the biggest buildings fill a pipe, late in an Era (§9).
  2. A level's goods come in like today's building materials: the gold is paid at once, the building waits under scaffolding for its goods from your storages, your spare, your Yard and, if you allow it, the market (§9).
  3. Research: masters' Hub Orders grow 80% a tier from tier 5; coal belongs to the Copper and Stone tracks, so there's no Coal track; the Power track counts half. Going deep now beats spreading for every track (§12).
  4. The Big Factory makes 28 ingots a minute instead of 32, in line with every other Era-1 building.
  5. The Era tree is in the numbers: copper pipes at Level 4, and 75 million gold in Era 2 for steel pipes, the outskirts survey, deep mining, power lines and silos (§12).
  6. The wilderness and other towns (§20): anyone builds trunk lines from Era 3 and runs them as a carrier; rights of way and outposts are bought once; a line is a second market at half the Hub Orders; the Lines track makes Pipe Masters; fog that expeditions map. Not in 1.4: seams that run dry, caravan camps, twin towns, joint projects, lodges.
  7. Company Level counts every coin you earn, daily orders and rewards included, and nothing else (§14).
  8. As in today's game: daily orders (three a day, about half an hour of income), storms and their repairs, managers at Levels 10, 20, 30 and 45 plus one from Automation (§19).
  9. Every plot has one trading post; its level is how you grow your market (§8).

Before that

  1. One balance at every size (rule 2): nothing depends on how many people play. No quiet-server bonus, no Wonder or Monument sized to active players, no "half the town" rule.
  2. Goods for levels: from level 11 a level step also needs goods worth 30% of its gold. It gives friends something to sell each other and Era-1 goods a use beyond the market.
  3. The Monument: paid in gold, with stages that never end, 40% dearer each; one site per Era. It's where the late-phase gold goes, and four friends pay for one.
  4. The Wonder: fixed sizes in goods (the Great Dynamo {{W1TEXT}} gold's worth), "buy and give" with gold at the market's 2.5×, each stage at least a day.
  5. The market: 1,000 g a minute of every Era-1 good, and the trading post's level as the way to grow it.
  6. Company Level from gold earned alone, and parcels at every second level from 5 to 27.
  7. Crew target lowered to +5–10% in week 1 and 10–20% after, from the model.
  8. Land: every plot has water, iron ore and wood; each lacks one of copper ore, coal, stone and sand, and one of bauxite, oil, rare earth and titanium ore.
  9. Research: two lines, the deep tracks and a short Era tree. Tracks are nine tiers over about ten days. Nothing gets cheaper because others researched it.
  10. The outskirts: nine parcels set aside for each plot from Era 2, rather than first-come land between the Yards.
  11. New Yards are open unless the first resident closes it. Workshops: each resident may build on up to 64 tiles of the Yard's ends.
  12. The end: each town launches its own Space Elevator and then does missions; there's no world-wide season ending.
  13. What goes: corporations (crews and towns instead), contracts (links instead), the Franchise and branch estates (moving and the outskirts instead), and the Bank, Showroom and Pneumatic Post.

Left open on purpose

  • The numbers of Eras 3 to 5: the Wonders after the Copper Cathedral, the Era tree's prices, the Monument sites, trunk lines, outposts and missions. They follow the same rules as Eras 1 and 2; the numbers in this document are where they start, and phase C sets them when the model plays those Eras. Phases A and B don't depend on them.
  • How it feels: whether the late phase of an Era is fun, and how fast real people play against the model. Only people can tell; the bots in phase B check the numbers first (§23).

The design is ready to build. Phase A starts when you say so.
