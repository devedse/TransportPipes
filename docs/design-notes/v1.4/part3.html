Each Era also opens more room: the outskirts in Era 2, the Deep Level in Era 3, the wilderness in Era 4. With levels capped at 25, land is how a plot keeps growing.

How a town moves up

  • Each town has its own Era. It moves up when its Era Wonder is finished (§13), and never by a deadline: an Era is earned. A town where nobody plays stays where it is.
  • The new Era starts for everyone living in the town, also those who are away.
  • Towns race each other on the world map: "Ironvale reached Era 3 first".

When the Era begins

  • The Era's buildings appear in the build menu, its deposits on your land wake up, and its tracks and Era research open.
  • The market buys and sells the new goods, and wants half as much again of the older ones.
  • A launch party in the hub: everyone in the town makes 10% more for a day. Loady opens the Era's chapter: what's new and where to start.
  • Your Yard gets a new Monument site.

Your Era and moving

  • Your Era is the highest Era a town reached while you lived in it. It never goes down.
  • Newcomers start in an Era-1 town. You can move to towns of your own Era or one below, never higher: to reach Era 3 you help a town get there.
  • In a town below your Era, your buildings from the higher Era sleep until the town catches up. Your research stays yours, but its higher-Era tracks only work on awake buildings. Moving down is for playing with newer friends, not for carrying their town.
  • Strong players can't rush a younger town's Wonder either: every stage takes at least a day to build once it's full, so an Era takes at least three days whoever pays for it.

How long an Era takes

An Era ends when the town finishes its Wonder, and Wonders have fixed sizes (rule 2), so a town with more active players gets there sooner. From the model (§17), for regular players who give half of their late-phase gold:

12 · Research

Research is where you choose what you're good at. It costs gold and goods when it starts, but mostly it costs time, and time can't be bought. The basic tiers take minutes, the big ones days.

Tracks

Deep research for one chain: its mine and its factories. Nine tiers over about ten days. This is what makes a specialist fifteen times better than someone who didn't research.

The Era tree

Short research that opens your Era's pipes, buildings and systems: minutes in Era 1, a day in Era 5.

  • Research needs a Lab (Level 4). Each of the two lines works on one thing at a time, with a queue of five, and keeps going while you're away. Two lines, so a two-day track tier never holds up a pipe you need now.
  • Research is yours. Your crew benefits by buying what your mastered chain makes.

  • Faster: the chain's buildings work faster. Everything they take and make goes up together, like extra levels, so a faster mine digs more from the same deposit.
  • Leaner: the chain's buildings need less of every input, energy included, for the same output. For wind, sun and water power it means 20% and 30% more output instead, for fuel-burning plants less fuel.
  • Complexes: four of the chain's 1×1 buildings in a square merge into one 2×2 building with the output of five; mega complexes: nine in a 3×3 square give the output of twelve. Bigger buildings get +25% and +33% instead.

Deep beats wide, for every track

The model (§17) played every Era-1 track mastered against the same plot researching every track a tier at a time. Going deep pays from the first week and keeps paying:

{{TABLE:tracks}}

Three rules make it so:

  • From tier 5, every tier also raises your Hub Orders for that track's goods by 80% (×19 at tier 9). The first four tiers make a chain better; the deep ones make the market want more of it, so a master isn't drowned by their own flood, and spreading thin doesn't get the bigger market.
  • Coal belongs to the Copper and Stone tracks, whose Smelters and Brickworks burn it: a Coal Pit follows the better of your two. So no track's chain waits on a track you haven't researched, and there is no Coal track of its own, which no plot could make pay.
  • The Power track counts half (each of its faster tiers gives half its boost: ×4.75 instead of ×15), because every building uses energy.

Before these rules, a Copper, Coal or Stone master earned 6–14% less than a player who spread their research, and only Iron clearly paid. Now the six tracks end within {{SPREAD:35}} times of each other by day 35; the aim is a third (1.33), and phase B's bots check it on the real server. The research panel shows what each tier adds in gold a minute, and Loady suggests your richest chain. In a crew, each resident going deep on a different track gives the Yard a master for every chain.

13 · Wonders and Monuments

Two kinds of shared project, and after the last Era a third:

The Monument · your Yard

Built with gold by the residents, in stages without end. Every stage makes every resident a little better. Four friends pay for one Monument and all four get it.

The Era Wonder · your town

Built with goods by everyone in the town, in three stages. Each stage gives the town a perk; the last brings the next Era.

Missions · after the Space Elevator

Launches without end, each a little bigger, each a lasting bonus and a star for the town.

Their sizes are fixed numbers, the same in every town and every Yard however many people play (rule 2). More people get them done sooner because more people make more.

The Era Wonder

  • Giving goods. Each good has a "give" switch with two settings: beyond my Hub Order (the spare the market would pay least for) or all spare. Either way it comes after your own buildings, links and Yard (§8). You can also give from your storages or the Yard's Depot at any time.
  • Giving gold. "Buy and give" buys the goods a stage still needs from the market at 2.5× and gives them: a way to turn gold into the next Era when nothing else is worth buying.
  • Giving is a gift: no gold comes back. You get a share of the rewards and the Era itself. The goods leave the game, which makes the Wonder the town's big sink.
  • A stage takes at least a day to build once it's full, so an Era takes at least three days whoever pays for it. Strong players can't rush a younger town.
  • The trail is blazed: each town that finished a Wonder before makes it 10% smaller for the towns after it, down to half. Late towns catch up.
  • Rewards by your share of the stage: Bronze 1%, Silver 3%, Gold 7%, Diamond 15% of what the stage needed. They pay 1, 3, 6 or 12 hours of your own income and a title; Diamond builders get a statue in the hub. Every giver's colour is on the Wonder; nobody is listed for not giving.
{{TABLE:wonders}}

Sizes are in gold's worth of goods at base prices. The first two are worked out in §17: the Great Dynamo is finished on day 12 by a town of eight regular players who give half of their late-phase gold, on day {{E2:16}} by sixteen and on day {{E2:1}} by one. The later ones follow the same rule and are checked in phase C. Every perk is for everyone in the town and lasts for good.

The Launch and missions

  • When a town's Space Elevator is finished, it launches: a countdown, a show in the hub, titles for every giver, statues for the Diamond builders, and Legacy for every player in the town (account perks for any future company: a manager slot, starting gold, cosmetics, never enough to outrun newcomers).
  • Then missions begin: each asks for Era-5 goods, 10% more than the last, starting at a fixed size set with the Space Elevator in phase C. Each one finished gives the town +1% production for good and a star on the world map. They never end, so the town's goods always have somewhere to go.
  • The world map ranks towns by Era, launches and stars.

The Monuments

Each Era gives the Yard one Monument site, in the Yard's square. The crew picks which of the five to build there, so by Era 5 a Yard has five, in its own order. A Monument is built with gold, by any resident, in stages without end: the first stage of an Era-1 Monument costs 100 million gold and each stage 40% more than the last. 14 · Progression, Era by Era

Company Level

Your Company Level is all the gold you've ever earned: what the market and other players paid you, daily orders, Wonder rewards and expeditions. Refunds, the start gold and buy-backs when you move don't count. Nothing else counts either, so it can't be farmed and it never goes down. Levels come every few minutes in the first hour, every day or two in Era 1 and every few days later. Each one unlocks something.

{{TABLE:levels}}

"Typical day" is when the regular player of §17 got there. Buildings of Eras 2–5 come with the town's Era, not with a level. After Level 25 each level needs 1.5 times the gold of the one before.

The journey

Era 1 · Steam & Iron · about 12 days in a town of eight

  • The first evening: from an empty plot to Level 6, about 170 buildings, copper pipes and the first chains (§17).
  • Days 1–4, building up: levels, a parcel every day or two, the first research tiers, complexes on your track. You pin "I make energy" on the Plan board.
  • Days 4–12, the late phase: the start area and the first parcels are built; the Monument's stages, the Great Dynamo's stages, a new parcel at every second level and your track's big tiers.
  • Day 12: the Dynamo's last stage. Era 2.

Decisions: which track, which parcel, which Monument, who in the Yard makes what, how much to give.

Era 2 · Electric · about three weeks

  • Steel needs ingots and coal, wire needs copper ingots: the Yard re-wires itself around the new chains.
  • Bauxite wakes on three of the four plots. Aluminium eats energy: the power specialist's big moment.
  • The outskirts: nine more parcels each, holding every resource, all bought in about a week.
  • Income nearly triples within four days, then the late phase again: a second Monument, the Copper Cathedral.

Era 3 · Oil & Machines · 3–4 weeks

  • Oil wakes in the deserts; refineries make plastic; gears and motors need three chains each.
  • The Deep Level under your plot: dig out parcels over days for rich ore, magma power and a huge Vault.
  • Trunk lines: links to players in other towns and a second market; the Pipe Master's role (§20).
  • The Terraformer: raise a hill, drain a lake, or seed the deposit your plot lacks.
  • The Grand Refinery Tower.

Era 4 · Electronics · 4–5 weeks

  • Rare earth; circuits, batteries and robots. A robot needs every land resource.
  • The wilderness opens: up to three outposts on rich deposits, Discoveries every four days, Quantum Links to bring it home.
  • The Thinking Machine.

Era 5 · Space Age · 5–6 weeks

  • Titanium from the mountains, superconductors, spaceframes; the Fusion Reactor.
  • Superconductor pipes: no limits, no losses.
  • The Space Elevator: the town's biggest project, then the Launch.

After the Launch

  • Missions without end, each a lasting bonus and a star.
  • Mastering the tracks you skipped; a perfect plot; decorations; the rankings.
  • Moving down an Era to play with newer friends (your higher buildings sleep).
