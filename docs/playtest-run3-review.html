TransportPipes · after AI playtest run 3 · three analysts, two rounds

Fix first, then one redesign: every want a buyer on the pipes

You asked a team of three analysts to look at run 3, bring a table of improvements, and say whether they'd focus on fixes or do another redesign. They reviewed the run apart, then read each other and voted again. All three end on the same answer: fix what froze and misled players now, run one cheap replay to check, then build one redesign before run 4. In that redesign, whatever needs materials (a building, an upgrade, the Era wonder) buys them through the pipes from you, your friends and the Yard, instead of pulling them from your own Storage. Friends see the need and can fill it with one tap.

The systems designerA mix Round 1 and round 2. "The fixes stop the game from lying and freezing; they cannot give friends a way to move a material, or gold a reason to exist."
The player researcherFixes → a mix Round 1: fixes only. Changed after checking the others' claims: "three of my late drops were not texts: the bricks, the Copper Pipe and the Dynamo sat behind a real wall."
The social designerA mix Round 1: a different redesign (Ask and answer, a one-off hand-over). Round 2: folded it into the systems designer's as its social side: "a manual hand-over already failed in this run."

What run 3 told them

Better than ever: the first two weeks

  • The early game is the best of three runs. Days 1–4: mean fun and clarity 3.79, "come back tomorrow" yes 12 of 14 (run 1: 8 of 12; run 2: 8 of 14).
  • The design's idea reached the screens once. Ana's stone ran Cleo's Brickworks for a week, both knew, and the playtest's first Thanks and Tends followed. The one "together 4" in the run.
  • One-tap fixes, one return card worked every time ("a real rescue").

Not chores

All three agree: upkeep was about a tenth of the commands, mostly one or two clicks, and nobody called it a chore. What wore players down was the same warning on return after return with no fix that worked, and nothing left to want.

Unchanged for three runs: weeks 3–5

  • Late game flat. From day 18: fun 3.0, "come back" yes 1 of 6, the same as runs 1 and 2.
  • The shared project froze on two rules. A House's capped quarter of water counted as a delivery, which switched the pass-over off; and the keystone rule chose water, a good nobody needed, in all four stations. The crew's Workshop sat at 96% for 27 days.
  • The game offered what its rules refused: 7 of 10 STUCK incidents ("forest" parcels with no wood, a Big Factory that needs 100 unmentioned bricks, "a Water Pump by the water" then "Not on water").
  • A real wall behind the late wants. Every bigger building, the Era 2 list and the Wonder need materials that can only come from your own Storage. Dealt land put them on a friend's estate, and nothing could carry them across. From day 15 to 36 the crew added 3 buildings and held 95–100% of all it earned.
  • The door was already there. The game has an Ask that buys from neighbours' spare and lets a House fill the rest. Its only entrance is a form under the Plan board. It was used 0 times in three runs; Ana's 100 bricks were one Ask away.
  • Ben was lost in 33 seconds and nothing brought him back.

Corrections they made to each other
  • Cleo's Coal Pit was bound by its output pipe, not by energy, and a second grey pipe adds nothing: capacity counts per producer–consumer pair (systems, correcting the player researcher).
  • Glass is not a two-friend chain, since energy is on every estate; Cleo made 12 bricks a minute, not 55 (social, correcting the systems designer).
  • The top bar's Era button did open the Wonders on Cleo's first try; it reads as a clock (player).
  • Mia's water failed because her pump had no energy from day 8 to 36, after the game's own fix pipe (player and social).

How this was made

Three analysts (Claude agents), each with one lens (docs/design-notes/after-run3/roles.md): the systems designer (the loop, the project, eras, the Yard, research and gold), the player researcher (what players saw, understood and felt; clarity, STUCK notes, chores, the phone) and the social designer (playing together: the crew, seats, gifts, the shared plan, the stranger). Same brief (brief.md): run 3's findings, digest, samples, players' logs and screenshots, runs 1 and 2 to compare, and the code to confirm causes and size changes.

  • Round 1 (r1-systems.md, r1-player.md, r1-social.md): each reviewed the run alone.
  • Round 2 (r2-*.md): each read the other two, checked the claims they adopted or disputed against the logs and code, and gave a final table and vote.

The run-3 report (playtest-run3-report.html) has the evidence behind every row: the sessions, diaries, screenshots and numbers.
