TransportPipes · multiplayer design · v1.2

Playing together

How three friends who join tonight play together, how a lonely player is never alone, what happens when people stop and come back, and how it holds from the first minute to the next chapter. One full design, decided by a team of six design agents in five rounds of brainstorm, sparring, exams, defence and a final review.

HousesLabelled NPC companies fill every gap (empty seats, friends not here yet, people who left) so nobody is ever stuck, and never pretend to be people.

Found along the way, and already fixed The economy agent found a live exploit while reading the code: contracts had no limit on the amount per minute, and tenants accepted any fair price, so a buying contract for 1,000,000 ingots a minute paid its buyer a shortfall penalty of 440,640,000 gold after one day (reproduced in a game test). Fixed in 9621766: a seller can promise only what its buildings make, and a penalty never takes gold below zero. The lonely-player agent found three things the code does today that this design removes: Sleep() ends every contract with a sleeping player, WakeUp() drops a returner on the first free seat, and the quiet-district check skips districts with fewer than 4 players.

1 · What the design must take into account

Before anyone brainstormed, we wrote down what every idea is scored against. The owner's core principle: it should be fun to play with three friends that join, ideally choosing a station or position; it must work for lonely players, for players who stop, and in the early and the late game.

#PointWhat it means
1Friends togetherThree friends who start together are neighbours from minute one, see each other, help each other and have something to decide together. A friend a week later can still join them.
2Choosing where you liveA player chooses a position with the information to choose well (who lives there, how active, what the land holds), with a sensible default.
3The lonely playerSomeone alone gets a complete game and a world that doesn't feel empty. Solo is never a handicap that makes them quit.
4Players who stopMost leave after days or weeks. No holes, dead partners, broken chains or blocked land; a returner can come back.
5The early gamePlaying together is easy to understand and immediately useful; nobody can hurt a newcomer; no dependency on others to progress.
6The late gameCooperation grows (bigger projects, longer logistics) and keeps veterans interested, with friendly competition.
7Asynchronous idle playNothing needs two players online; an idle partner never stalls you.
8Fair and safeNo griefing, blocking, leeching or gold funnelling; veterans can't crush newcomers.
9ClearYou can always see who you play with, what goes where and why, ideally on the map.
10Pipes are the heartPlaying together is expressed through pipes and logistics.
11Scale and performanceWorks with 5 players and with 2,000; no district-wide mega-networks.
12Chapters, no wipeWorks across chapters and migrates the testers' world.
13Social glueRecognition, shared goals, identity, a reason to invite.
14BuildableShips in steps, each one playable.

The veto rules

The guardian of lonely players turned the list into rules that can veto any idea. Every rule in this document passes them:

  • Every partner can be replaced: no pipe ends nowhere; a link with no flow always says why.
  • Solo is a full game: a player without human partners earns 85–90% of a crewed one, and nothing needs a number of humans.
  • Nothing needs two players online: offers wait 72 h, deadlines at least 12 h, nothing rewards being online at one moment.
  • Absence costs nobody else: at most 5% of a partner's income on any day, at most the solo gap in the long run, no lost multipliers, no bigger baskets. Nobody is moved without their own tap, except an asleep estate (30+ days) into safety and a stranger on probation in a crew's station (§6).
  • NPCs are honest: labelled everywhere, never on leaderboards, at most 25% of any shared goal.
  • Nudges vanish when nobody is there: every push towards others switches off where there are no others.

2 · The design in one page

You live in a station: four estates around a small shared Yard. You pick it on a map that shows who lives there and what the land holds; friends come along as a crew and an invite is a held seat. Lay your own pipe to the Yard and your spare goods go to the neighbours whose buildings need them, at one fixed price, while your factories buy their spare the same way; what nobody needs goes to the hub as before. Everyone else you reach with links: two-ended pipes you can see, between two named companies. Empty seats, friends not yet here and people who left are run by labelled NPC Houses, so nobody is ever stuck. Your station builds its project together, and later your crew lays trunk lines in its colours to far hubs.

Where you liveA station of 4 seats, or a small one of 2. You choose it, and you can move once a week.
Who you play withA crew of 1–8 friends from Level 1, one banner. No wallet, no votes; leave in one tap.
How goods moveThe Yard: your station, one fixed price. Links: two named ends, anyone else. Never a merged network.
Built togetherThe station's project, crew trunks, Discoveries, Wonders and leagues. Credit is your own share.

Six rules every feature follows

  1. Together makes you better, never stuck, guilty or prey. Playing with people adds about 10–15%, never access.
  2. Absence costs others at most a small, announced step: a sleeping partner's trades keep running at their old terms, then glide to NPC terms with notice, never more than 5% in a day.
  3. Nobody presses a button that feels like kicking a friend. No votes, no removal, no release.
  4. Every choice can be undone or recovered from: moving, leaving, a link; a lapsed seat comes back as the first landing card whenever it is still free.
  5. Contact between players is positive only: help, trade, thanks; never taking or breaking.
  6. Every zero has a sentence, and every push towards others switches off where there are none.

4 · Places and choosing where you live

Nothing on the map moves and no tester loses land. Each district stays a 5×5 grid around its hub: the four corner 2×2 groups become stations of four seats and the four pairs on the arms become small stations of two (still 24 seats). Each has its Yard at the inner corridor crossing.

How a district is used

A station: 4 seats around a Yard.

A small station: 2 seats around a small Yard, for couples and for anyone who wants fewer neighbours.

A Yard at the corridor crossing (its layout is below).

A crew of 7–8: the NE station and both its small stations (N and E), joined by game-drawn crew pipes (dashed) into one big station with one Yard pool and one project. A crew of 5–6 takes the station and one small station.

A new district opens when the open ones are 60% lit or held, or when a crew starting with friends finds no station with enough free seats. New players fill the free seats of one district before the next gets any.

Districts with no lit seat freeze: no simulation, nothing lost; they wake when someone lands.

The Yard's layout

A Yard is 10×10 tiles: the 2×2 corridor crossing, the four 2×4 corridor arms and a 4×4 corner of each of the four estates (16 of a corner parcel's 64 tiles, 1.6% of the estate; corner parcels unlock only at Levels 35–50). Corridor tiles stay corridor: links pass under the Yard in covered channels. One corner holds the sockets (one per seat), one the notice board and the start of the ring pipe, and the two opposite corners the project, which grows from a 4×4 Foundation to a Landmark across both with an arch over the crossing. Nobody builds in the Yard. Your Yard gate is on the edge of your start area nearest the Yard; the game draws a gate strip from it across your locked corner parcel to your socket, so at minute 12 you lay only 8–16 tiles of your own pipe. Every Yard picture is made for these footprints and checked with fit.mjs.

  • From your second visit the cards also show links waiting for a seat, the project's stage and Houses' goods.
  • Strangers see activity as buckets ("lively", "some days", "resting"), never "0 of 7 days" next to a name.
  • "Find me neighbours" seats you in the station with the most active humans within 10 levels of you; ties go to a waiting link, then to a House that makes a good you lack. Never an all-NPC station while a station with an active human has a free seat.
  • Moving is free in your first 3 days, then free once a week (an hour in transit, as today). A crew moves as a block: the first member's tap reserves seats for the whole crew for 7 days, and each member moves with their own tap. Leaving a crew gives one extra free move, at most once per 30 days.

The asleep friends' holds move too: their estates (already asleep) are packed and their held seats point to the new station, so "Your seat" on their return is next to their friends.

6 · Crews

  • Founding takes 15 seconds from Level 1: a name, one of 40 emblems, two colours. No crew levels to grind, no shared wallet. Crew and company names pass a word filter, and anyone can report one; three reports from different stations hide the name ("Company 4127") until it is reviewed.
  • Size 1–8. 2 → a small station; 3–4 → a station; 5–6 → a station and one of its small stations; 7–8 → a station and both. The crew pipes between them are drawn by the game, lose nothing and carry the Yard's tier, so the friends in the small stations are full members of one big station. Beyond 8: a corporation, which stays as it is (the 30-player layer).
  • Seat codes. Every held seat has its own code, and the share sheet always sends one, so an invited friend moves in at any time with nobody online. The general crew link (for friends without a held seat) needs a member's tap after 24 h, so a link posted publicly can't hand a stranger a friend's seat.
  • Seats held for friends who haven't arrived stay held while any member played in the last 14 days, up to 28 days, warmed by a House. Nobody taps "renew".
  • Leaving is one tap and changes no price; you keep your seat and estate and simply become a neighbour.
  • Nobody can be removed. No votes. Anyone can privately Block any company: the Yard stops trading between those two after 1 hour (covered by the tank), and gifts, asks and messages between them stop. The other side reads "X now trades its spare with others". If companies that made up more than half of your Yard trade last week have blocked you, you get one card: "Your goods sell better elsewhere: move for free to one of these stations that need them". Nobody learns who blocked.
  • A station where one crew lives in or holds 3 or more seats is closed to strangers by default. Any active member can open or close it; closing never moves anyone who already lives there.
  • Probation applies only to a stranger who takes a free seat in a station where a crew lives in or holds 2 or more seats: away 5 days in a row within their first 14, the estate is packed to the Quiet Isles with nothing lost, its trades pass to a stand-in at once, and on return the old seat is the first landing card if it is still free. Everywhere else a newcomer's absence follows the normal timeline (§10).
  • Seeing each other: crew colours on roofs, gate flags and pipe stripes; one morning report and the notice board carry the crew's news, naming goods and totals, never a person ("the crew's copper saved the crew 840 g/h").
Friends who fall out (day 10)Ben leaves the crew in one tap; no prices change and Ben's credit stays Ben's. Ana blocks Ben; the Yard keeps trading with everyone else. Either may move for free. No vote, no announcement, no kick.

7 · The Yard and links: how goods move between players

How the Yard works

  • You lay your own pipe from your network to your Yard gate: that's your consent.
  • The Yard is not a buyer. It matches residents: what one resident's buildings lack is filled from another resident's spare, at 1.75 × the hub's base price, both ways. Spare that nobody needs right now goes on down your flow order (the project, storage, the hub). The Yard never holds stock and never creates a price for goods nobody wants.
  • Under the hood it's the teleporter: one buyer and one seller inside each estate's own network. No network ever crosses a gate. At most 8 sockets.
  • Your pipe's tier is its capacity per connection, all goods together: grey 100/min, copper 500, steel 5,000. Your own pipe's loss is yours: a seller is paid for what reaches the Yard.
  • The ring pipe around the Yard belongs to the station: one stripe per seat in its crew's colours (Houses grey), growing lamps, arches and the Landmark with the project.

Who gets what when a good is short

  • Buyers: usual buyers first (each buyer's share of this good over the last 7 days, crew or not), then crew mates, then new demand in proportion to need. A usual share shrinks by at most 10% a week, so nobody's factory loses its supply overnight because a neighbour built more.
  • Sellers, when there is more spare than need: usual sellers first (their 7-day share), then crew mates, then in proportion to spare.
  • Houses in the station take part at House prices (§9) and always come after humans.

Links: everyone outside your station

  • A link joins exactly two named companies, gate to gate, and is never transitive. Goods never cross a third company's estate. Links are auto-routed through the corridors and drawn as bundles; nobody owns a corridor tile, so nobody can block one.
  • You offer a link; the other side accepts with one tap (offers wait 72 h; Houses accept at once). At most 5 offers an hour, at most 12 links per estate.
  • Every link has a 2-hour tank at the buyer's gate, a 1-hour notice for pauses, a fallback (the Yard, then the hub) and one state line, so no pipe ever ends in silence.
  • Loss is the pipe's: grey 0.3% a tile, copper 0.15%, steel 0.05%, so distance and better pipes matter.
  • Flow order for a producer's spare: own consumers → contracts → open Asks → links → the Yard (residents) → the project → storage keeps → hub. A "project first" switch per good puts the project ahead of the Yard.
  • A coupling is re-sent at most once a minute and only when it moves more than 1%, so rings of estates settle in a few rounds instead of rippling (the lesson of the 500-round solve).

8 · Prices and the money rules

"Players pay each other one fair price; the hub always buys, less for floods; Houses help a little." Prices are multiples of the hub's base price of the good.

RuleValueWhy
One player price1.75 × base, both ways: the Yard, links, contracts and the ExchangeFriends never haggle; they decide who makes what. Strangers can't be fleeced, newcomers can't be lowballed, and no price funnels gold to an alt.
The Hub OrderToday's price curve, made personal: per company and per hub, the hub pays base × clamp(√(D ÷ your sold rate), 0.25, 1.0), where D is the hub's demand per company for that good (× 1.5 per Era after the first, × quiet grace) and your sold rate is your own one-hour average. The bar shows D: up to it you get the full price, beyond it every extra unit earns less.Nobody's sales move anybody else's price, so nobody can crash a newcomer's market. The balance runs are single-company, so they already play this curve: the level pacing stays as tuned. A second hub pays √2 on a split volume good, which makes trunks worth it (§13).
Quiet graceD ×2 while the server has fewer than 50 active companies, ×4 below 10. Never per district.A push towards others vanishes when there are none, and nobody is paid for settling away from people.
The Port0.6 × base for any tier-2+ good, no limit, reached only by pipeThe valve for very big producers: it beats the hub only once you sell about 2.8× your Hub Order there.
HousesBuy at 1.25 × the price the hub pays you for that good right now (never above 1.25 × base); sell at 2.0 × baseAlways a little better than the hub, always worse than a friend. New gold from Houses is at most 0.25 × 25% ≈ 6% of what the hub pays you.

ContractsPromise only what you make (done). A seller who hasn't played for 72 h before a shortfall is absent: the contract pauses (the buyer's tank and fallback cover it) and is never charged. No shortfall clauses below Level 6A holiday never costs money, logging out never beats delivering, and a newcomer can't be trapped.

Solo parity: a solo player sells to Houses at 1.25 × their own hub price and buys from them at 2.0; a crewed player sells to friends at 1.75 and buys at 1.75, as much as the friends' buildings draw. The target is a gap of 10–15%: worth inviting a friend, never worth quitting over. The balance runs measure it when Houses ship (step 4), and the Houses' volume is tuned to hit it.

9 · Houses and stand-ins: the NPC companies

Today's tenants become Houses: named NPC companies with a portrait and a ⚙ badge, their card saying "House (NPC)". One system fills every gap: empty seats, seats held for friends, and the trades of people who sleep.

RuleValue
KindsReliable, Grower, Collector, Neighbour in need. No "Rival": contact is positive only.
StrengthBuilding levels = the median level of the station's active humans − 3 (at least 5). Tier 1–3 goods (tier 3 only from Era 2), never 4–5.
PricesBuy at 1.25 × the price the hub pays you (never above 1.25 × base), sell at 2.0 × base.
One total capHouses buy only what their works use. All NPCs together (Houses, stand-ins, Ask fills) buy at most 25% of a company's output of a good and sell at most 25% of its need. When the cap is full, an expired Ask is filled by the hub at 2.5 and still closes.
HonestyNever on a leaderboard, never more than 25% of any shared goal, at most one letter a day. They always send a returner one welcome postcard.
Warming a seatWhen a human arrives, the House leaves a starter kit (3–5 buildings at level ≤ 5) and hands over its links, then moves out with a note.

When someone sleeps: the stand-in

From day 30 an asleep estate produces and earns nothing (as on the Quiet Isles today), whether it is held in place or packed. Its trades (links, contracts and its Yard flows) pass that minute to a stand-in House, announced to every partner on day 23, so there is no day on which they stop. The gold of a stand-in's trades is NPC gold: it is removed from the game, never the owner's. The rules are split by direction, so the friend who stays isn't hurt and an alt can't farm it:

  • When the partner buys from the stand-in: 100% of the sleeper's 14-day median, at the old price, for 14 days; then the volume falls 10% a week while the price stays. The partner pays what it paid before; this side makes no gold (it's a sink), so it sits outside the 25% cap.
  • When the partner sells to the stand-in: the old price for 7 days if the sleeper is a crew member with 5+ distinct days of play on a different device; then, for everyone, the price glides over 14 days to House terms, and the volume above the 25% cap moves to the Yard and the hub over the same 14 days. Never more than 5% of the partner's income in one day.
The fake-quitter farm, closedAn alt that builds demand for a week and then "quits" used to mint 15–25 million gold (up to 4.5 days of its owner's income, by the economy exam). Now the alt has to buy at 1.75 with its own gold first, the stand-in buys inside one 25% cap that doesn't stack with more alts, and the old price lasts 7 days: at most about 1¾ days of income per 44-day cycle (5 days played, 30 away, the handover) per verified account. The friend who stays keeps over 90% of their income through a handover.

  • Holds end by themselves. Nobody can release a hold, so there is no button that sends a friend away. Seven days before a hold ends, every active crew member gets one card: "Keep Ben's seat 60 more days?" (the first answer counts, once). Without an answer it ends: the estate travels safely to the Quiet Isles and a House warms the seat. All holds lapse when no crew member has been active for 14 days.
  • Seats of players without a crew are reserved to day 37. After that a House warms them, and placement gives such a seat to a newcomer only when it is the last free seat in its station, so the lonely don't lose their one neighbour first.
  • Absence costs nobody else. A basket is sized when its stage starts and counts only delivering residents, so a friend who stops makes the next basket smaller even while their estate keeps trading. Nothing anyone sets (not even a Block) changes a basket.
  • The code fixes come first: Sleep() hands trades to the stand-in instead of ending them, WakeUp() shows landing cards instead of the first free seat, and the quiet-district check counts active seats, not players.
  • A message budget: the in-game morning report and at most one e-mail a day in a window the player picks (no web push in this version); to an absent player at most 3 messages in all, each with something good, never a countdown.

11 · Newcomers, lonely players and small servers

The newcomer in week 3

  • "Find me neighbours" seats the newcomer next to active humans, or in the Frontier: the newest open district, offered only when it has at least 3 active seats of players who started in the last 14 days (it never opens a district by itself).
  • Everything the veterans' buildings lack sells at 1.75 from minute 12; there is no other price, so nobody can lowball a newcomer. Credit in the project is a share of your own output, so 20% of a Level-6 output counts as much as 20% of a Level-30 one.
  • The personal Hub Order means no veteran's flood lowers the newcomer's hub price.
  • Tailwind ×2.5 in week 3, an Era jump kit, and a league of 25 at the newcomer's own stage, which a newcomer can win.

The lonely player, at 3 a.m. on a quiet server

  • Placement seats Mia next to an active human, Jo (never an all-NPC station while a human has a free seat). Mia's sand sells to Jo's Glassworks through the Yard in minute 12, while Jo sleeps.
  • Houses fill the other seats, clearly labelled, at prices worse than a friend's: Mia gets a full game, 85–90% of a crewed player's income, and no pretence. Mia's project basket is sized for Mia alone, and Houses deliver up to a quarter of it.
  • On a server of 5: quiet grace ×4, public copper trunks to the next hubs and the Port, and the district map shows who was here today, never how few are online now.
  • On a server shrinking from 200 to 20: placement fills the liveliest districts, the lone-human and consolidation cards bring people together, crews move as a block, empty districts freeze.

Small mode: the testers' server

Below 50 active companies: new players fill one district first; leagues and Discovery brackets are off; Houses fill every empty seat; quiet grace ×2 (×4 below 10). The measures of §21 are read from talking to every tester, not from percentages.

Visible life without being online together

Lit windows on estates played in the last day, pellets in their sellers' colours in the Yard, the notice board's daily line, postcards, and "usually here: evenings" on banners. Contact is cheap and positive: tend a friend's estate, ask for a good, pin what you make on the Plan board.

12 · Doing things together: the project, asks and tending

The station's project

Each station builds one project over the chapter: Foundation → Water Tower → Workshop → Foundry Hall → Landmark. The project is a buyer in the Yard: it takes what its basket lacks from the residents' spare and pays the hub's full price (1.0 × base), so delivering never loses money and needs no button.

Asks, the Plan board and tending

  • Ask for… a good, for 12–72 h (72 by default). Crew mates and neighbours fill it whenever they play; at the deadline a House fills what's left at 2.0 (the hub at 2.5 when the NPC cap is full), so an Ask always closes. An Ask can renew itself ("Cleo's Glassworks always gets my spare sand first").

  • Tending: once a day, a one-tap favour on a friend's estate; it banks for 3 days and shows when they come back ("your crew tended your estate on 17 days", never who). It carries no stat bonus, so it can't be farmed.

13 · The long game and the next chapter

The balance runs show income flat from day 14 to 21 (+2%). The long game makes growth come from crossing borders: logistics, the game's heart. Recomputed by the economy reviewer, the pieces below give a crew about +15–25% from day 14 to 21, and a solo player on public trunks +10–20%.

WhenWhat
Week 2Era recipes re-wire the Yard; the personal Hub Order makes one-product estates look outward.
Week 3Crew trunks to a second hub (its own Hub Order: a split volume good earns about +39%), the Port and outposts, whose ore feeds the Yard. The Foundry Hall (+10% on two chains) lands by day 18–19. Discoveries every 4 days, announced 24 h ahead and lasting at least 7 days.
Week 4The Landmark; the District Wonder and the Era Wonder, fed by trunks, with stages sized like the project's baskets, so a district of one human still finishes its stage. Era Wonder tiers by the share of your own Era output delivered: Bronze 1%, Silver 3%, Gold 7%, Diamond 15%.
CompetitionLeagues of about 25 companies at a similar stage, regrouped weekly with no demotion (off in small mode). Every station that pipes to a Discovery within 72 h gets its marker; the three that deliver most in those 72 hours, per stage bracket, are named on it, so being online when it is announced decides nothing.

Trunk lines

  • A crew trunk is a steel pipe along the public corridor to another hub, the Port or an outpost, built in 10-tile segments: any member pays a segment (2 h of T(L) at their level, plus 300 steel and 100 concrete), shown in their colour. Pump houses every 25 tiles are part of it.
  • It carries the crew members' own goods and their links. No tolls, no rented lanes, no upkeep. Like links, trunks are bundles: no corridor tile is ever owned, and a parallel trunk is always possible.
  • Public copper trunks join every hub to its neighbours and the Port, for everyone, at a 2% fee that is removed from the game, so a solo player always reaches far markets. When every member of a crew is asleep, its trunk becomes public.

The next chapter

Everything persists: crews, estates, stations, trunks, held seats, Legacy. The Landmark becomes an heirloom; the project restarts with a new recipe; leagues and Discoveries start again; a Homecoming week, a fresh Frontier, and an opt-in move for a whole crew.

DecisionKeep the 32×32 estate and the 16×16 start area with the surveyors' small ★2 patches, so the first hour works alone and nobody depends on a friend for their first Smelter. For new estates, the seat's two rich home goods (★5 and ★4, for example copper ★5 and coal ★4 at Copper Gulch) lie in the first two parcels you can buy (Level 5 and 7), visible from minute one behind the fence. Existing estates keep their land; their specialties count as home goods.
Change it if: the median time to the first parcel exceeds a day, or TheFirstDay reaches Level 10 later than day 2.

15 · Performance

  • No network crosses a gate. Every estate solves on its own; the district mega-network (68 buildings and 1,050 pipe tiles in one tester's game, 380 ms a solve) can't come back.
  • The Yard pool, links and trunks are teleporter-style terms filled from the last solve: the cost grows with estates + links + pool members, not with the district.
  • Couplings are damped (re-sent at most once a minute, only above 1% change), so a ring of estates settles in at most 5 rounds.
  • Asleep estates are not simulated; stand-ins and Houses are supply tables updated every 15 minutes. Districts with no lit seat freeze.
  • Wonders, leagues and the notice board are ledgers.
  • Caps: 8 Yard sockets, 12 links per estate.

Change it if: a Yard solve exceeds 5 ms at 8 members, or a re-solve exceeds 20 ms on the biggest tester estate (then cap links at 8).

No wipe and nobody moves. Dry-run on a copy of the database first, with a snapshot to roll back to. Each item runs in the build step named after it (§19).

  2. Links first (step 2): every human pair with flow in the last 7 days gets a link between their nearest gates; then other corridor pipe chains between two gates become links, and the rest are refunded.   3. The Hub Order (step 2) replaces the global price reaction, with a balance run first.

20 · What must never happen

  • A pipe that carries nothing and says nothing.
  • A reward that needs two players online, or counts how many humans showed up.
  • Anyone gaining from someone else's absence.
  • A vote, a kick, a release, or a list of who didn't deliver.
  • A leaver blocking a seat, a corridor or a friend for longer than the rules say.
  • An NPC pretending to be a person, or on a leaderboard.
  • Gold moving between players other than as the price of goods really made.
  • A newcomer as prey: lowballed, trapped in a contract, outcompeted in a league.
  • A return that starts with a mess, a scolding or a debt.
  • Pressure in notifications; more than one message a day.

Appendix B · What players feel, and what other games taught

Eight things players feel

  1. Relationships need a choice. A neighbour you were assigned who trades with you automatically feels like the water company. That is why the district mega-network failed: nobody chose anybody.
  2. Small groups carry the warmth, big groups the spectacle. The 3–5 you know, the 15–50 you recognise, the server.
  3. Helping feels better than being helped, when it costs little and is thanked. Being helped too much feels like debt.
  4. Absence must not hurt anyone. Being the reason someone lost (guilt) or being stalled by someone who isn't there (resentment) kills groups.
  5. People avoid empty restaurants. A world that looks busy keeps even solo players.
  6. Comparison should be local. 3rd of 25 is exciting; 500th of 2,000 is depressing.
  7. Return shame is real. A mess or a scolding waiting at home keeps people away for months.
  8. Inequity beats greed. A deal that feels unfair is refused even when it pays.

The verdict: the small group is right, but the round-1 version carried four jobs at once (where you live, who your friends are, who you trade with, the shared project) and broke for couples, groups of six, friends who fall out and strangers placed together. The final design keeps them as separate things: the station is a place, the crew is people, the Yard and links are trade, and the project is what you build.

Appendix D · Three rival concepts and the exams

A · Stations with a YardStations of 4 and halts of 2 around a game-owned Yard. Laying your own pipe to the Yard trades your surplus with all residents at one fixed price, strangers included. No votes, no kicks; leavers become profiles.
B · Streets & LinesA crew lives along one corridor and shares the Main, a pipe the game lays there for members only. It grows lamps and arches with crew projects and becomes a Line across the map.
C · Places, Crews, LinksThree clean layers: places, crews (people who chose each other), bilateral links. Strangers share the project and the view, never a market.

The mergeThe outsider's summary became the plan: C's skeleton (place, people and trade as separate things), A's manners (the open Yard, no votes, a Handshake that waits for you, absent sellers never fined), and B's soul (the crew's stripes on a pipe that grows lamps and arches, "usual buyers first", damping). Plus the economy examiner's caps, which close every funnel and farm it found.

What decided the hard questions

  • Do strangers share the Yard? Yes. It is the one rule that gives a lonely player at 02:40 a human buyer without waiting for anyone (C's 72-hour offer fails that; B's members-only pipe runs past strangers' doors). The fears ("a stranger takes a couple's coal") are answered by usual-buyers-first, and a couple can live in a small station.
  • Votes? None. A vote among four friends is kick drama (Hay Day, Egg, Inc.). Leaving is one tap; Block is private.
  • What happens to a long-absent estate? It stays in place for the crew, but asleep: not simulated, earning nothing, its trades run by a stand-in. Simulating the absent for months is a farm, costs CPU and makes stations look full while they're dead.
  • One price or several? One. Friend and Premium prices gave friends a choice nobody needed and alts a funnel; "who makes what" is the decision worth having.
